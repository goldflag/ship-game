# Runtime flight discrepancy register

This supplements the static ship discrepancy register for the shared carrier-aircraft runtime. The September 2026 flight revision changes simulation and animation only; it does not change the Enterprise blueprint, any aircraft mesh, or the historical claims attached to those assets.

- Coordinated bank-driven turns, finite roll/pitch/throttle response, dive speed changes and sea pullout anticipation are bounded gameplay rules. They omit lift/drag polars, stall/spin dynamics, angle of attack, propeller torque, wind and air density. Role envelopes are tuning values, not measured F4F/SBD/TBD performance.
- Fighter acquisition runs every 0.65 seconds with persistent targets and a carrier defense bias. Bullet lead uses relative velocity and a nominal 720 m/s speed. Damage remains an abstract aimed burst, without individually simulated bullets, visibility/occlusion sensing, pilot skill or probabilistic dispersion. Nearby allies block firing lanes. Break turns and overshoot separation are heuristics; there is no aircraft collision solver.
- Dive bombers form up at staggered heights, seek a ballistic release during descent and pull away from unsuccessful runs. Torpedo bombers approach from the nearer beam, descend, and require a stable wings-level release; their aim includes the torpedo's fall before its water run. Two unsuccessful passes lead to return with the payload retained. Neither attack doctrine nor release envelopes are a historical reconstruction.
- Patrol and recovery use position-driven circuits. Recovery captures the extended centerline, reserves the landing lane, leads a moving deck and goes around after an unacceptable approach. Gear, hook, control surfaces and split dive brakes use the existing independent joints with gradual travel. No physical arresting cable, deck-contact solver, flap mechanism, wing folding or elevator operations are added.
- Approach circuits increase sortie duration. In the stationary 5 km Enterprise fixture, first bomb and torpedo releases occurred at approximately 120 s and 111 s; the last aircraft completed landing at approximately 514 s and 513 s. Fleet traffic, target maneuvers and combat change these times. The existing 650 s endurance limit remains a gameplay constraint; fighter patrols now recall at 260 s to leave time for recovery.
- Export checks establish asset consistency only. Validation measurements and browser evidence belong in `assets/reviews/aircraft-flight/`; they do not certify historical accuracy.

## Faster launch and distant visibility follow-up

Launch taxi is now 22 m/s, with a 3.6-second, 140 m deck run and 2.4-second climb-out phase. A following taxi can begin one second after the preceding plane leaves the deck. These accelerated timings are gameplay tuning; they do not establish historical taxi, catapult or launch performance. Return taxi and deck lane exclusion remain in place. The previous sortie timestamps above describe the earlier slower launch revision.

The renderer supplements aircraft with a seven-pixel distant silhouette as projected wingspan drops below 14 pixels. It remains depth tested and fogged, and fades away when zoom resolves the model. This is a visibility aid for subpixel geometry, not optical recognition or a physical enlargement of the aircraft. Its explicit distance fade is 16–20 km, in addition to map fog. Follow-up pixel and timing evidence is retained in `assets/reviews/aircraft-flight/visibility-launch/`.
