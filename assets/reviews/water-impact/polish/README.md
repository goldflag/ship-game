# Shell water impacts — final animation review

The splash rises as an irregular, dense water column with a low outward crown. Its rounded masses separate at the crest and shrink into fine falling rain. Light mist drifts downwind, and returning water briefly renews the foam on the displaced ocean. The incoming shell direction, caliber and CPU impact height shape the effect. Day, storm and moon lighting affect water already in the air.

This replaces the first-pass sheet geometry, whose straight edges and repeated strips were too visible. The [earlier review](../README.md) remains as historical evidence. Launch speeds, dimensions and breakup timing are authored visual approximations.

## Rendering budget

The water body uses one instanced draw and fixed storage for 16 impacts, with at most 512 parcels each: 8,192 quads / 16,384 triangles. Only visible, airborne parcels are submitted. Positions follow analytic gravity and drag, without per-parcel objects or matrices. Projection-based detail reduction retains the mass at distance and restores detail under binocular zoom; offscreen effects continue aging. Fine droplets and wind mist use separate bounded batches with offscreen and subpixel culling. Total combat-effect particle capacity is 11,168.

The porous texture is generated by existing procedural code. The effect adds no liquid raymarch, extra depth or ocean pass, runtime fluid solver, or downloaded texture. Returning-water foam uses the existing bounded atlas and displaced-ocean shading.

## Measured performance

The [paired water-only GPU report](gpu-effects.json) compares the final implementation with PR #80's original sheet implementation, commit `065055956463752d2f693ebf7fc2ec6147babe95`. The renderer reported Apple / Metal 3. Each scenario used 40 alternating paired samples after eight warmup frames, at 1920 × 1080 with the same camera, events, textures, fog and render-target settings. GPU timestamps exclude browser scheduling and readback waits.

| Scenario | Original GPU median | Final GPU median | Original CPU publication | Final CPU publication |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 8 nearby impacts | 0.852 ms | 0.983 ms | 0.86 ms | 0.53 ms |
| 32 nearby impacts | 1.638 ms | 1.966 ms | 1.30 ms | 1.11 ms |
| 32 impacts falling | 1.769 ms | 0.786 ms | 0.81 ms | 0.66 ms |
| 32 distant impacts | 1.180 ms | 0.852 ms | 1.25 ms | 0.19 ms |
| 32 impacts through binoculars | 1.180 ms | 1.376 ms | 1.27 ms | 1.03 ms |
| 32 offscreen impacts | 0.459 ms | 0.459 ms | 1.25 ms | 0.04 ms |

The 32-impact scenarios deliberately saturate the 16-body pool. Nearby eight-shell GPU cost increased by 0.13 ms; the difference of medians for the close saturation case was 0.33 ms. Its paired median increase was 0.26 ms and paired p90 increase was 0.72 ms. CPU publication improved in every case. Publication timings average ten paused `effects.update(..., 0, camera)` calls per sample; they exclude simulation and are not full animated-frame CPU timings.

The [whole-scene comparison](gpu-scene.json) retains the ocean, sky, ships and postprocessing in the timed render. It uses 24 paired samples and explicit node-frame advancement so the pass cannot reuse a cached image. Whole-scene samples varied by tens of milliseconds, much more than the isolated effect cost, so they cannot reliably attribute an FPS change to the splash. These frozen render probes also exclude ocean simulation updates. Timings are local measurements, with hardware, driver and scene dependence.

Run the probes without another active renderer or build workload.

## Validation

[69 focused tests](tests.txt) pass, covering gravity and reentry at the CPU water height, 30/60/144 FPS trajectories, camera-independent positions, caliber and entry angle, pause/reset, saturated pools, distance/zoom visibility, existing smoke behavior, returning-water foam and shell combat. [The production build](build.txt) passes ship/aircraft checks, TypeScript and Vite; the existing large-chunk warning remains.

The [GPU regression report](gpu-regression.json), produced by `scripts/diagnostics/water-plume-render.html`, checks the production geometry and material under WebGPU and WebGL, with standard and reversed depth. All four configurations render growing instance buffers, freeze exactly when paused, disappear behind opaque geometry, respond to light changes, clear on reset and render again after reset without GPU validation errors.
