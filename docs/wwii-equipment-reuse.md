# WWII fleet equipment reuse

This is a library planning guide, not approval of any ship's geometry or historical configuration. Exact vessel, date, mount mark, shielding and refit still control component selection. National labels below describe service use, not necessarily the weapon's designer or manufacturer.

For a roster spanning all major WWII surface fleets, light AA offers the broadest reuse. Main batteries are usually shared within classes or related classes; a destroyer's main gun can also be a larger ship's secondary/AA gun. No single complete turret covers every ship of a country across the entire war.

| Navy | High-value families to build | How broad is the overlap? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| United States | 5-inch/38 Mk 12; 40 mm Bofors; 20 mm Oerlikon | The 5-inch/38 is the strongest heavy-gun example: fitted to nearly every major US warship built 1934–1948, with several distinct single/twin mounts. Wartime Bofors/Oerlikon fittings are widespread but depend on date. Older ships and smaller escorts are exceptions. [5-inch family](https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNUS_5-38_mk12.php), [US families](https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNUS_Main.php) |
| Japan | 25 mm Type 96; 127 mm/40 Type 89 | Type 96 is the closest fleet-wide AA family. Type 89 serves many large ships but is not the 127 mm/50 Type 3 destroyer gun. Keep single/twin/triple and shield variants separate. [Type 96](https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNJAP_25mm-60_mg.php), [Type 89](https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNJAP_5-40_t89.php) |
| Britain / Commonwealth | 2-pounder pom-poms; 20 mm Oerlikon; 40 mm Bofors; 4-inch AA families | Pom-poms are especially widespread on major warships; Oerlikons and Bofors broaden wartime coverage. There is no direct equivalent to one US 5-inch/38 family across all ship types. Single and multiple pom-poms can use different gun marks. [Pom-pom](https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNBR_2pounder_m8.php), [British families](https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNBR_Main.php) |
| Germany | 2 cm C/30 and C/38 families; 3.7 cm SK C/30 and later automatic AA | Broad light-AA reuse, but C/30/C/38 and older semiautomatic versus later automatic 3.7 cm guns need distinct mechanisms and mounts. [German families](https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNGER_Main.php), [SK C/30](https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNGER_37mm-83_skc30.php) |
| Italy | Breda 20 mm/65 and 37 mm/54; separate Scotti 20 mm families | Large returns from light AA; do not treat all Italian 20 mm weapons or all model years as identical. [Breda 20 mm](https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNIT_20mm-65.php), [Italian families](https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNIT_Main.php) |
| France | Hotchkiss 13.2 mm, 37 mm families; Allied Bofors/Oerlikon refits | Early French outfits and Allied-refitted ships need separate date-specific collections. US/UK-refitted ships can reuse Allied weapon families, with appropriate mounts. [French families](https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNFR_Main.php) |
| Soviet Union | 37 mm 70-K; 12.7 mm DShK; 130 mm families for relevant classes | Strong wartime AA reuse. Older ships, imported equipment and different 130 mm gun/mount designs prevent one universal assembly. [Soviet families](https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNRussian_Main.php) |

Treat these as family-level prioritization, not evidence that every named weapon fits a particular ship. Model mechanical subassemblies once where justified, register explicit mount variants, then author each ship's supporting platform and installation. A complete fleet requires a matrix of class and refit date, not just country.
