# Carrier aircraft operations

Enterprise's version-1 `airWing` is authored in its ship blueprint and compiled through the normal ship pipeline. Its finite inventory is **48 aircraft: 18 Wildcats, 18 Dauntlesses and 12 Devastators**, with six-plane flights, four active flights and 12 deck positions. All ready aircraft start in the hangar. Their existing GLBs remain independent asset-pipeline outputs. This reduced mixed group is gameplay tuning, not a historical manifest.

## Battlefield camera and squadron commands

**M** switches the existing perspective camera to a high, north-up view of the actual ocean scene, tilted 20° from vertical. No modal or separate chart replaces it. Ship targets, squadron tags, the selected route and station markers use the same perspective projection as the scene. There is no grid. Drag or use arrow keys to pan, scroll or use −/+ to zoom. Fit battlefield frames both fleets; Center carrier recenters the camera. Zoom preserves the water point under the cursor. M or Return to ship restores the ship camera; Esc first cancels a pending target action and otherwise restores the ship camera and its optics; pause, port/reset and following an aircraft retain their existing behavior. Combat continues while overhead, with ship aiming and firing suspended.

The authored squadron pools are divided into stable `flightSize` groups at runtime: Enterprise has three fighter, three dive-bomber and two torpedo-bomber squadrons of six. Their IDs derive from the carrier ID, authored pool ID and group index. Their plane membership never changes; losses reduce the surviving count without refilling from another group. Compact 90 px cards use baked transparent images of each group’s actual aircraft model, without a repeated selection heading or instructional block. Mission, payload, endurance and condition details remain available through Aircraft inspection or hover titles. The bottom row exists in both camera modes; click a box to select it, or use 1–8 in M view. Number keys continue selecting ship weapons outside M view. Ready, Launching, On mission, Returning, Servicing and Lost remain mutually exclusive aircraft totals.

A selected ready squadron launches with its first valid order. An active squadron receives the same order through the shared CPU validation path:

- Right-click water: all roles fly to and loiter around a station within 30 km of their carrier. Bombers keep their payloads.
- Right-click an enemy ship: armed dive or torpedo bombers strike it.
- Right-click a friendly ship: fighters defend that ship, following its movement.
- Right-click an enemy squadron: fighters intercept that specific squadron.
- Right-click another friendly squadron: fighters escort it.
- Return squadron recalls just the selected group; Recall all cancels queued launches and returns airborne survivors.

In M view, **L** arms loiter, **A** strike, **D** defend, **I** intercept and **E** escort; click a compatible target to issue the order. Fighter/bomber role controls show the available actions and their keys. **R** returns the selected squadron, **X** recalls all, and **V** toggles individual aircraft inspection. These shortcuts are contextual to the battlefield and leave the normal ship controls available outside it. Esc or pressing the selected action again cancels targeting. Right-click still supplies the contextual target order when no action is pending.

Invalid targets, wrong roles, exhausted armament, servicing squadrons, low endurance and unavailable launch slots reject commands without partially launching or changing the previous order. The selected order survives targeting another ship. Missing defended or escorted targets fall back to carrier defense. A destroyed intercept target becomes a loiter order at the fighter's current area; destroyed ship strike targets return with unused payload.

Name tags show remaining aircraft and actual activity (Launching, En route, Loitering, Attacking, Engaging, Defending, Intercepting, Returning or Servicing) in the ship and M cameras. Fixed loiter-station crosses and circles exist only in M view; their water positions come from the persistent order, independently of each plane's orbit. Aircraft details retain condition, endurance, recovery queue and follow controls. Lost and hidden hangar aircraft cannot be followed.

## Deck and flight simulation

All 48 aircraft start hidden in the hangar. A valid launch queues only the chosen squadron. When released, an aircraft appears at the authored launch datum, unfolds its wings during the 3.6-second run and completes a 2.4-second initial climb, then flies its mission. A shared carrier cadence of `(10 - run - climb) / (flightSize - 1)` spaces departures: a six-plane group completes launch in approximately ten seconds with a clear deck. Overlapping runs are intentional gameplay tuning. A following squadron waits for the launch cadence; aircraft stay hidden while queued. There is no global count cap on aircraft in takeoff or flight. Carrier damage, approaching landings and the authored per-carrier flight/deck rules can suspend launches.

Survivors use the existing downwind, base and final approach circuit with emergency endurance priority, a compressed 60 m separation on final and a 1.2-second deceleration after touchdown. They then transfer into the hangar for the authored 35-second service interval and return to their original squadron slot. Recall during the deck run clears the runway before service. Service repairs survivors and restores ammunition and payloads; losses never respawn. The deck/hangar transfer is abstract, without elevators, physical arresting cables or a deck collision solver. These cadence and recovery settings are explicitly gameplay choices.

Turns develop through finite roll rates and bank-driven lateral lift; pitch, throttle, dive speed and pullouts have finite response. Damage, gun bursts and time-based endurance remain deterministic gameplay rules. The earlier stationary, AA-disabled traffic fixture recovered 24 planes by 663 seconds; those timestamps describe the previous launch/recovery cadence. The current regression still requires all 24 aircraft to recover, rearm and relaunch without endurance losses or exceeding 12 deck aircraft. This does not establish sortie timing under moving-carrier combat; see the [current command validation record](../assets/reviews/carrier-command/README.md) and [earlier traffic measurements](../assets/reviews/carrier-operations/README.md).

Fighter bursts require a relative-velocity bullet lead solution, 0.12 seconds of steady aim, a narrow forward gun cone and a clear friendly-aircraft lane within 600 m. Fighters ease their speed in a tail chase and break away from close passes or a fighter on their tail. Supplied, surviving high-angle ship guns fire finite, seeded AA bursts, including at returning flights. Static and moving target AI remains passive. AA shares mount training, ammunition, reload, damage and obstruction rules with surface gunnery and takes priority while an airborne threat is in range. Damage resolves from burst miss distance; it is not a full simulation of each AA projectile or fuze. Dive bombers stage at separate heights, commit to a descent with dive brakes, and seek a ballistic release solution. Torpedo bombers line up from the nearer beam, settle into a low, level run, and include the payload fall in their intercept. Both can abort an unsafe pass, reposition, and try once more before returning with the payload retained; misses remain misses. Their HE payloads use existing swept ship hits, armor, fragmentation, equipment damage and flood-space rules. Dropped torpedoes fall with gravity, then transition to straight 23 m/s runners at 2 m depth, arm after 180 m and expire at 4.5 km. Friendly hulls can intercept weapons, but do not award hostile damage points. Aircraft strikes attribute ship damage and later frags to the carrier; air kills are separate squadron counters.

The renderer loads three published LODs per supported model, batches each rigid component with instancing, and animates stable wing-fold, propeller, gear, hook, rudder, aileron, elevator and dive-brake IDs. Attitude and mechanism travel interpolate between CPU ticks, including angle wrap; the follow camera samples that same attitude. Gear and hook deploy gradually, ailerons respond to roll input, and split dive brakes open during an actual bombing descent. It displays finned bombs that turn into their falling trajectory, falling torpedoes, and short moving fighter and ship AA tracers. Fighter effects retain the captured gun direction, airframe velocity and attitude; ship AA starts at the actual muzzle and uses its registered speed and endpoint. Shot-down aircraft leave combat immediately, then their CPU wreck poses descend under drag and gravity into one sea-impact event. Smoke follows the wreck path and sea impacts generate spray and foam; normal combat effects show bomb and torpedo impacts. LOD transitions use 120 and 400 m. A nine-pixel silhouette fades in across 48–14 pixels of projected wingspan, overlapping the thin model before it becomes difficult to see from a follow camera. The former 14-pixel threshold left a visibility gap for edge-on aircraft. It respects scene depth and fog, fades out as binoculars resolve the real model, and is hidden for deck/lost aircraft and inspection. The silhouette expands into additional small instanced draws as needed; it does not change aircraft size or hits in simulation. GPU waves never drive aircraft or weapon outcomes.

Remaining approximations: wreck descent and tracer spacing are visual/gameplay approximations; wrecks meet the flat gameplay sea without terrain/hull collisions, secondary damage or breakup. Ordnance shapes and wing gun locations are original approximations rather than documented historical reconstructions. No full aerodynamic model (including stalls or wind), physical arresting cables, deck collision solver, elevator operations, historical air-group manifest, blast damage to aircraft, rescue/diversion, user-piloted cockpit, manual bombing reticle, or historical torpedo failure rates. Fighter ammunition is counted in bursts. Endurance is a time budget. Losing a carrier still participates in ship-based battle victory; airborne survivors cannot resurrect it. See the [Enterprise runtime flight discrepancy register](../assets/ships/enterprise-cv6/reports/flight-discrepancies.md) for tuning and provenance limitations.

Registered high-angle guns up to 130 mm now engage hostile airborne aircraft automatically. Bismarck has eight twin 105 mm, eight twin 37 mm and twelve single 20 mm AA mounts connected to its existing original models. Guns train and elevate, fire visible traveling tracers, consume their shared ammunition, and respect damage, magazine supply, obstruction and friendly-lane checks. The two upper quad 20 mm fittings remain decorative. Effective ranges and burst damage are gameplay calibration; heavy AA uses an approximate timed-burst hit radius. Other ships participate according to their registered guns; visual-only fittings do not supply invisible AA damage.

Individual aircraft can be followed from Air operations, including visible parked, taxiing and returning planes. Ready or queued aircraft inside the hangar have a disabled Follow control. The chase camera samples the same displayed hull pose and interpolated airborne position as the renderer. Move the mouse to orbit the followed aircraft; with the cursor released, drag either mouse button or touch instead. Scroll adjusts follow distance from 12–800 m. The same controls work during shell follow. Orbit and zoom preserve the ship’s saved camera, aim and binocular magnification. The camera continues tracking the actual displayed target, clears sea/terrain, and holds its chosen relative offset through translation. Aircraft follow suspends shell follow and hides the ship reticle; mouse clicks operate the follow camera without firing or opening optics. Return to ship, camera/recenter controls, port/reset and aircraft loss end tracking; pause holds the flight pose. This is observation, not player flight control.

Aircraft accuracy uses the battle seed, stable aircraft identity, sortie and attack pass. Fighter bursts sample angular aim error and can miss even after acquiring a firing solution. Strike pilots hold an imperfect aim point for the entire pass: provisional standard deviations are 24 m across the ship and 45 m along it for bombs, 24 m across and 100 m along for torpedoes. These are gameplay settings, not historical hit rates. Bombs inherit actual aircraft velocity and use swept ballistic collision; torpedoes retain their release heading. Different seeds produce both physical hits and misses; identical seeds replay identically. Launching another squadron does not reroll an existing aircraft’s aim.
