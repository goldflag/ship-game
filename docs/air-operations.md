# Carrier aircraft operations

Enterprise's version-1 airWing is authored in its ship blueprint and compiled through the normal ship pipeline. The initial inventory is six Wildcats, six Dauntlesses and six Devastators. Their existing GLBs remain independent asset-pipeline outputs. This reduced mixed group is gameplay tuning.

The Air group instrument launches three available planes per click. Strike aircraft retain the selected hostile surface target. Fighters seek nearby hostile aircraft, then patrol between the carrier and target. Recall cancels queued planes and orders airborne survivors home. Bot carriers launch one group of each role and relaunch surviving aircraft after recovery. Inventory is finite; lost aircraft never respawn during a battle. Returning aircraft rearm and repair in 35 seconds after parking when the service module is available. The flight inspector shows HP, phase, bursts and payloads; the chart shows team-colored aircraft crosses.

The renderer-free fixed tick owns the phase sequence: ready → queued → taxi → takeoff → outbound/attack → returning → landing → rollout → parking → rearming → ready, or lost. Aircraft taxi from stable parking spots to the authored launch datum, accelerate along the deck, and climb away. One aircraft at a time uses the flight lane; the authored three-second minimum also applies, but taxi/run time normally makes launches 16–22 seconds apart and recovery occupies the lane for about 27–32 seconds per plane. Returning planes wait for the lane, descend to the actual tyre datum, decelerate for three seconds, and taxi back to their own spots. Turning, altitude seeking, damage, gun bursts and flight endurance are deterministic gameplay rules. At most 144 aircraft are airborne across all carriers; queued launches wait for capacity. All 18 surviving aircraft are visible on deck in port and battle when parked, queued or rearming. Deck aircraft follow the displayed hull pose, including roll and pitch. Parking is a compact single row derived from the existing flight-deck datums; aircraft have no deck collision bodies. Aircraft use original engine-shaft origins; tyre clearances are taken from the published deck sockets. Ship roll, pitch, immersion and the service magazine can suspend launches/recovery. Port/reset clears all flights, weapons and losses; pause freezes simulation and articulation.

Fighter bursts damage only hostile aircraft within range and an aim cone. Supplied, surviving light ship guns provide a bounded AA damage envelope, including on returning flights. AA is abstract and does not train or consume the mounts' surface-gunnery ammunition. Bombers seek a ballistic release solution; misses remain misses. Their HE payloads use existing swept ship hits, armor, fragmentation, equipment damage and flood-space rules. Dropped torpedoes fall with gravity, then transition to straight 23 m/s runners at 2 m depth, arm after 180 m and expire at 4.5 km. Friendly hulls can intercept weapons, but do not award hostile damage points. Aircraft strikes attribute ship damage and later frags to the carrier; air kills are separate squadron counters.

The renderer loads three published LODs per supported model, batches each rigid component with instancing, and animates stable propeller, gear, aileron, elevator and dive-brake IDs. It displays payloads, falling torpedoes, fighter tracers and brief loss trails; normal combat effects show bomb and torpedo impacts. LOD transitions use 120 and 400 m. GPU waves never drive aircraft or weapon outcomes.

Remaining approximations: no aerodynamic model, physical arresting cables, deck collision solver, elevator operations, wing folding, historical air-group manifest, blast damage to aircraft, rescue/diversion, user-piloted cockpit, manual bombing reticle, or historical torpedo failure rates. Fighter ammunition is counted in bursts. Endurance is a time budget. Losing a carrier still participates in ship-based battle victory; airborne survivors cannot resurrect it. See the Enterprise discrepancy register for tuning and provenance limitations.

AA coverage in this first implementation comes only from registered guns of 40 mm or smaller: Enterprise and Type VIIC have eligible mounts. Bismarck, Yamato and Baltimore currently have no simulated AA envelope; they need friendly carrier fighters for air protection. Adding their historical AA batteries remains future authoring work.

Individual aircraft can be followed from the Air group list, including parked, taxiing and returning planes. The chase camera samples the same displayed hull pose and interpolated airborne position as the renderer. It preserves ship aim, suspends shell follow, and hides the ship reticle while active. Return to ship, camera/recenter controls, port/reset and aircraft loss end tracking; pause holds the flight pose. This is observation, not player flight control.
