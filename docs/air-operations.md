# Carrier aircraft operations

Carriers use the same version-1 `airWing` in their ship blueprints and compile through the normal ship pipeline. Enterprise's finite inventory is **48 aircraft: 16 Wildcats, 16 Dauntlesses and 16 Devastators**, with legacy Custom/online six-plane flights, four active flights and 12 deck positions. All ready aircraft start in the hangar. Their existing GLBs remain independent asset-pipeline outputs. This reduced mixed group is gameplay tuning, not a historical manifest.

Shōkaku's December 1941 preset uses the same global gameplay complement: 48 aircraft, comprising 16 A6M2 Zeros, 16 D3A1 Vals and 16 B5N2 Kates. This deliberately reduced equal-role inventory is not a historical manifest. Each carrier's legacy six-plane limit produces nine groups (6, 6 and 4 aircraft per role); deck and concurrent-flight rules remain shared. The PvE four-plane grouping and deck policy are tracked in the [PvE implementation status](pve-implementation-status.md). The published Japanese aircraft rigs retain their wings in flight position; A6M2 and B5N2 historical folding hinges are not yet modeled. D3A1 has fixed landing gear and wings. Wildcat and Devastator folding remains driven by their authored hinges, and capability is checked against every exported LOD. The renderer loads the aircraft needed by the selected fleet, with three LODs each, and retains cached models when the fleet changes. See the [Shōkaku reference and discrepancy record](../assets/ships/shokaku/README.md) for configuration uncertainty.

## Battlefield camera and squadron commands

The ocean covers the full map view through maximum zoom and panning, including portrait windows. Position and camera tilt ease together in both directions, and reversing a transition starts from the currently displayed pose. The development review at `/scripts/diagnostics/carrier-camera.html` opens the actual renderer at maximum zoom; `carrierCameraReview` exposes frame stepping for inspecting transitions.

**M** switches the existing perspective camera to a high, north-up view of the actual ocean scene, tilted 20° from vertical. The camera rises into the map and descends back to the ship over 1.4 seconds; reduced-motion preferences keep the switch immediate. Map markers and routes track the rendered camera every frame in logical HUD coordinates, so automatic and manual HUD scaling keep them aligned with the scene. Pointer picking and edge panning use viewport pixels. No modal or separate chart replaces it. Ship targets, squadron tags, the selected route and station markers use the same perspective projection as the scene. Route and station-ring segments are clipped to the camera view before perspective division, so targets behind the camera cannot reverse their direction when zooming. Ship nametags use compact 80 × 5 px hull-HP bars with percentages centered below and no vessel-status text. Sunk ships retain only a faded name and cannot receive squadron orders. There is no grid. Command/Ctrl-drag or middle-mouse drag to orbit; Shift+arrow keys also change the angle. Orbit adjusts tilt from overhead to 80° from vertical. **Shift-drag** draws a box around your airborne squadron positions to select them together. **Command/Ctrl-click** a squadron card, map label or manifest row to add or remove it; Command/Ctrl+Shift-drag adds a box to the selection. A normal click selects one squadron, or deselects it when it is the only selection. Orders and Return apply to every selected squadron through the existing role, readiness and flight-slot checks; feedback reports partial acceptance. Selected groups share gold highlights and show their routes. Camera dragging, arrow keys, edge panning, zoom and orbit remain available when **H** hides the instruments. Drag, hold arrow keys, or move the mouse within 32 pixels of a screen edge to pan; scroll to zoom. Camera buttons and the tilt slider are omitted to keep the sea visible. The map zoom radius runs from 300 m to 40 km, allowing twice the previous maximum zoom-in. Zoom preserves the water point under the cursor; near the horizon or over the sky, navigation uses a stable water anchor below the horizon. M or Return to ship restores the ship camera; Esc first cancels a pending target action and otherwise restores the ship camera and its optics; pause, port/reset and following an aircraft retain their existing behavior. Combat continues while overhead, with ship aiming and firing suspended.

The authored squadron pools are divided into stable `flightSize` groups at runtime: both carriers have three groups per role under legacy rules, containing six, six and four aircraft. Their IDs derive from the carrier ID, authored pool ID and group index. After all survivors reach the hangar, depleted groups of the same model and role combine when their combined surviving count fits the authored flight size. The earlier group keeps its ID, the merged group leaves the command roster, and remaining cards show their current numbered shortcuts. Aircraft keep their individual health, service timers, ammunition and IDs; losses never respawn. Compact 90 px cards use baked transparent images of each group’s actual aircraft model, without a repeated selection heading or instructional block. Per-aircraft numbers and follow controls remain available through Aircraft inspection or hover titles. The squadron row appears only in M view; click a box to select it, or use 1–0 for the first ten flights. Flights beyond ten remain selectable in the squadron row and manifest. The wing manifest at the right edge of the M view lists every squadron by role with one cell per aircraft: the cell fills with condition in the aircraft's status color and its underline shows the remaining bomb or torpedo, or the fighter's gun bursts out of `FIGHTER_AMMO_BURSTS`. Its header reports surviving aircraft, active flights against `maxActiveFlights`, deck occupancy against capacity, hangar and recovery-queue counts and a deck-suspended warning; each row shows the squadron's activity, mission and endurance or rearm timer, and clicking a row selects the squadron. The manifest can be hidden from its header, disappears with H, and is omitted at widths of 600 px and below. Clicking the sole selected box or map label, or pressing its number again, deselects it and cancels pending targeting. The normal carrier view retains the gun selection, ammunition and firing controls, with an Air wing card beside ammunition showing the configured M shortcut and opening the map. Number keys continue selecting ship weapons outside M view. Ready, Launching, On mission, Returning, Servicing and Lost remain mutually exclusive aircraft totals.

A selected ready squadron launches with its first valid order. An active squadron receives the same order through the shared CPU validation path:

- Right-click water: all roles fly to and loiter around a station within 30 km of their carrier. Bombers keep their payloads.
- Right-click an enemy ship: armed dive or torpedo bombers strike it.
- Right-click a friendly ship: fighters defend that ship, following its movement.
- Right-click an enemy squadron: fighters intercept that specific squadron.
- Right-click another friendly squadron: fighters escort it.
- Return recalls the selected groups; Recall all cancels queued launches and returns airborne survivors.

In M view, **L** arms loiter, **A** strike, **D** defend, **I** intercept and **E** escort; click a compatible target to issue the order. Fighter/bomber role controls show the available actions and their keys. **R** returns the selected squadrons, **X** recalls all, and **V** toggles individual aircraft inspection. These shortcuts are contextual to the battlefield and leave the normal ship controls available outside it. Esc or pressing the selected action again cancels targeting. Right-click still supplies the contextual target order when no action is pending.

Invalid targets, wrong roles, exhausted armament, servicing squadrons, low endurance and unavailable launch slots reject commands without partially launching or changing the previous order. The selected order survives targeting another ship. Missing defended or escorted targets fall back to carrier defense. A destroyed intercept target becomes a loiter order at the fighter's current area; destroyed ship strike targets return with unused payload.

Name-tag positions and visibility follow interpolated airborne squadron centers and the camera every frame in both views; status text retains the 10 Hz telemetry cadence. Name tags show remaining aircraft and actual activity (Launching, En route, Loitering, Attacking, Engaging, Defending, Intercepting, Returning or Servicing) in the ship and M cameras. Fixed loiter-station crosses and circles exist only in M view; their water positions come from the persistent order, independently of each plane's orbit. Aircraft details retain condition, endurance, recovery queue and follow controls. Lost and hidden hangar aircraft cannot be followed.

## Deck and flight simulation

All ready aircraft start hidden in the hangar. A valid launch queues only the chosen squadron. When released, an aircraft appears at the authored launch datum, deploys any authored folding wings during the 3.6-second run and completes a 2.4-second initial climb, then flies its mission. A shared carrier cadence of `(10 - run - climb) / (flightSize - 1)` spaces departures: a six-plane group completes launch in approximately ten seconds with a clear deck. Overlapping runs are intentional gameplay tuning. A following squadron waits for the launch cadence; aircraft stay hidden while queued. There is no global count cap on aircraft in takeoff or flight. Carrier damage, approaching landings and the authored per-carrier flight/deck rules can suspend launches.

Survivors use the existing downwind, base and final approach circuit with emergency endurance priority, a compressed 60 m separation on final and a 1.2-second deceleration after touchdown. They then transfer into the hangar for the authored 35-second base service interval. Damage extends this by the missing-health fraction: 80 HP takes 42 seconds, 40 HP takes 56 seconds and 10 HP takes 66.5 seconds. Recall during the deck run clears the runway before service. Service repairs survivors up to 60 HP, preserving health already above 60, and restores ammunition and payloads; losses never respawn. The deck/hangar transfer is abstract, without elevators, physical arresting cables or a deck collision solver. These cadence and recovery settings are explicitly gameplay choices.

Squadrons assemble into a close, shallow V after launch. Stable aircraft slots sit 32 m apart laterally and 27 m behind each preceding row, with a small height step. Wingmen follow the leader's moving and rotating slot using finite steering and throttle corrections; the leader leaves turning margin for the outside wing. Gentle seeded drift (up to 1.5 m sideways, 2 m fore/aft and 0.7 m vertically) keeps the formation alive without frame-dependent jitter. Surviving aircraft keep their slots when a new leader takes over. Patrol, defense and escort transit use the formation; fighters break off for individual gun solutions and evasive maneuvers, then regroup. Bombers share an approach heading and start the attack run with their leader, separating only for final individual ballistic/intercept corrections (inside approximately 1.7 km for dive bombers and 2.1 km for torpedo bombers). Releases retain seeded aim error and friendly-lane checks; formation flying does not guarantee hits. Recall and recovery use individual landing circuits. The development page `/scripts/diagnostics/aircraft-formation.html` shows the actual rendered flight; add `?squadron=vt-6&attack` for a torpedo strike and use `formationReview.advance(seconds)` to inspect later poses.

Turns develop through finite roll rates and bank-driven lateral lift; pitch, throttle, dive speed and pullouts have finite response. Damage, gun bursts and time-based endurance remain deterministic gameplay rules. The earlier stationary, AA-disabled traffic fixture recovered 24 planes by 663 seconds; those timestamps describe the previous launch/recovery cadence. The current regression still requires all 24 aircraft to recover, rearm and relaunch without endurance losses or exceeding 12 deck aircraft. This does not establish sortie timing under moving-carrier combat; see the [current command validation record](../assets/reviews/carrier-command/README.md) and [earlier traffic measurements](../assets/reviews/carrier-operations/README.md).

Disciplined fighter bursts require a relative-velocity bullet lead solution, 0.12 seconds of steady aim, a narrow forward gun cone and a clear friendly-aircraft lane within 600 m. Fighters ease their speed in a tail chase and break away from close passes or a fighter on their tail. Supplied, surviving high-angle ship guns fire finite, seeded AA bursts, including at returning flights. Static and moving target AI remains passive. AA shares mount training, ammunition, reload, damage and obstruction rules with surface gunnery and takes priority while an airborne threat is in range. Damage resolves from burst miss distance; it is not a full simulation of each AA projectile or fuze. Dive bombers stage together with a 3 km approach, finish their turn toward the target at altitude, then commit within 1.6 km with dive brakes and seek a ballistic release solution. The approach capture radius accommodates their finite turning radius, including close launches and crossing targets. Torpedo bombers line up from the nearer beam, settle into a low, level run, and include the payload fall in their intercept. Both can abort an unsafe pass, reposition, and try once more before returning with the payload retained; misses remain misses. Their HE payloads use existing swept ship hits, armor, fragmentation, equipment damage and flood-space rules. Dropped torpedoes fall with gravity, then transition to straight runners at 2 m depth and arm after 180 m. The Devastator retains its 23 m/s, 4.5 km gameplay torpedo; the Kate uses a Type 91 Mod 2 at a nominal 42 knots with a 2 km range. The Val carries a Type 99 No. 25 250 kg bomb with a nominal 60 kg filling. Shared arming, contact fuzes, damage and reliability remain game calibration; the Val bomb does not reproduce its historical delayed semi-armor-piercing behavior. Payload selection follows the aircraft model, including in mixed fleets. Friendly hulls can intercept weapons, but do not award hostile damage points. Aircraft strikes attribute ship damage and later frags to the carrier; air kills are separate squadron counters.

The renderer loads three published LODs per supported model, batches each rigid component with instancing, and animates stable wing-fold, propeller, gear, hook, rudder, aileron, elevator and dive-brake IDs. Attitude and mechanism travel interpolate between CPU ticks, including angle wrap; the follow camera samples that same attitude. Gear and hook deploy gradually, ailerons respond to roll input, and split dive brakes open during an actual bombing descent. It displays finned bombs that turn into their falling trajectory, falling torpedoes, and short moving fighter and ship AA tracers. Fighter effects retain the captured gun direction, airframe velocity and attitude; ship AA starts at the actual muzzle and follows its captured launch direction, inherited ship velocity and gravity. Light AA tracers continue for at least three seconds and at least one second beyond the sampled target flight time, with a 0.65-second fade; reaching the sea ends visibility. Ship AA firing snapshots carry the fitted caliber. Tracer cores, envelopes, tips and exposure lengths scale with caliber, so light AA reads as fine, short streaks and heavy AA stays distinguishable. Heavy AA tracers terminate at the CPU-sampled flight time and endpoint, where a brief fireball throws narrow fragments and radial gas fingers. The fragments burn out within 0.38 seconds; the fireball cools within roughly half a second into three overlapping charcoal smoke volumes that expand, drift with wind and disperse over roughly six seconds. The gas fingers disperse within 1.3 seconds. All phases reuse the bounded smoke and ignition pools; late frames sample the burst’s current age without replaying its flash. See the [AA fragmentation and shell-size review](../assets/effects/naval/reports/aa-fragmentation/README.md). Flak remains visible in binoculars, including bursts fired by the player. The renderer retains observed shots independently of the 128-event combat log, and tracer instance storage grows in pages instead of dropping shots at 512. Pause freezes both travel and smoke; reset clears active shots and pending bursts. The development review at `/scripts/diagnostics/anti-aircraft-effects.html` samples travel, burst, drift and expiry on the actual ocean scene. Shot-down aircraft leave combat immediately, then their CPU wreck poses descend under drag and gravity into one sea-impact event. Smoke follows the wreck path and sea impacts generate spray and foam; normal combat effects show bomb and torpedo impacts. LOD transitions use projected wingspan thresholds of 90 and 28 pixels. The lowest-detail model remains rendered below nine pixels rather than being culled by size. A faint, soft contact hint fades in below 12 pixels of projected wingspan to support tiny edge-on geometry. Its size is capped at three pixels and never exceeds the projected wingspan; maximum opacity is 28%. It shrinks with the aircraft and leaves resolved airframes and their paint untouched. The contact has no distance cutoff, respects scene depth and fog, fades out as binoculars resolve the real model, and is hidden for deck/lost aircraft and inspection. Contacts expand into additional small instanced draws as needed; they do not change aircraft size or hits in simulation. GPU waves never drive aircraft or weapon outcomes.

Remaining approximations: wreck descent and tracer spacing are visual/gameplay approximations; wrecks meet the flat gameplay sea without terrain/hull collisions, secondary damage or breakup. Ordnance shapes and wing gun locations are original approximations rather than documented historical reconstructions. No full aerodynamic model (including stalls or wind), physical arresting cables, deck collision solver, elevator operations, historical air-group manifest, blast damage to aircraft, rescue/diversion, user-piloted cockpit, manual bombing reticle, or historical torpedo failure rates. Fighter ammunition is counted in bursts. Endurance is a time budget. Losing a carrier still participates in ship-based battle victory; airborne survivors cannot resurrect it. See the [Enterprise runtime flight discrepancy register](../assets/ships/enterprise-cv6/reports/flight-discrepancies.md) for tuning and provenance limitations.

Registered high-angle guns up to 130 mm now engage hostile airborne aircraft automatically. Bismarck has eight twin 105 mm, eight twin 37 mm and twelve single 20 mm AA mounts connected to its existing original models. Unselected groups operate automatically; a directly selected gun group follows the player’s manual aim and fire commands. Guns train and elevate, fire visible traveling tracers, consume their shared ammunition, and respect damage, magazine supply, obstruction and friendly-lane checks. The two upper quad 20 mm fittings remain decorative. Effective ranges and burst damage are gameplay calibration; heavy AA uses an approximate timed-burst hit radius. Other ships participate according to their registered guns; visual-only fittings do not supply invisible AA damage.

Individual aircraft can be followed from Air operations, including visible parked, taxiing and returning planes. Ready or queued aircraft inside the hangar have a disabled Follow control. The chase camera samples the same displayed hull pose and interpolated airborne position as the renderer. Move the mouse to orbit the followed aircraft; with the cursor released, drag either mouse button or touch instead. Scroll adjusts follow distance from 12–800 m. The same controls work during shell follow. Orbit and zoom preserve the ship’s saved camera, aim and binocular magnification. The camera continues tracking the actual displayed target, clears sea/terrain, and holds its chosen relative offset through translation. Aircraft follow suspends shell follow and hides the ship reticle; mouse clicks operate the follow camera without firing or opening optics. Return to ship, camera/recenter controls, port/reset and aircraft loss end tracking; pause holds the flight pose. This is observation, not player flight control.

Aircraft accuracy uses the battle seed, stable aircraft identity, sortie and attack pass. Fighter bursts sample angular aim error and can miss even after acquiring a firing solution. Strike pilots hold an imperfect aim point for the entire pass: provisional standard deviations are 24 m across the ship and 45 m along it for bombs, 24 m across and 100 m along for torpedoes. These are gameplay settings, not historical hit rates. Bombs inherit actual aircraft velocity and use swept ballistic collision; torpedoes retain their release heading. Different seeds produce both physical hits and misses; identical seeds replay identically. Launching another squadron does not reroll an existing aircraft’s aim.

## Air gunnery tuning and panic

All aircraft retain 100 HP. Successful ship AA checks deal 8 HP at up to 25 mm, 16 HP above 25–80 mm, and 40 HP above 80 mm: respectively thirteen, seven, or three successful hits to destroy a fresh aircraft. Fighter bursts have an 80 HP damage budget, reduced by alignment and range (roughly 15–80 HP per successful burst). Their 16-burst ammunition supply and 0.4-second cadence are unchanged. Aircraft return below 25 HP; service repairs them up to 60 HP and preserves health already above that ceiling.

Ship AA angular aim error is doubled from the initial panic-fire tuning, and AA damage is reduced by 60%. Ideal, centered spread-only estimates at 1 km are approximately 0.15% for light/medium AA and 0.8% for heavy AA per barrel check. Fighter angular aim error retains the initial panic-fire tuning. Wings-level fighter bursts at a predicted aim-point distance of 400 m have about a 3.1% chance of hitting. Actual engagements depend on movement, training, banking and firing opportunities. These remain seeded gameplay burst checks, not historical per-projectile hit rates or a simulation of every bullet. Bomb and torpedo accuracy is unchanged. Tuning lives in `src/simulation/airGunnery.ts`.

Automatic AA crews and fighter pilots can enter 2–4-second panic episodes while engaging hostile aircraft, followed by 4–7 seconds of recovery before another episode can begin. At each decision, the provisional chance rises from 18% to 60% with pressure: damaged mounts/pilots and close inbound threats to AA or nearby fighter targets. Episodes use independent crew/aircraft identity and the battle seed, persist in serializable CPU state, and stop when contact is lost.

Panicked AA crews discard their velocity lead and train toward a wrong patch of sky, offset 10–34 degrees horizontally and up to about 21 degrees vertically, bounded above the horizon. Mount traverse, elevation, obstruction, reload and ammunition rules still apply. Panicked fighter pilots fire prematurely within a wider forward cone after 0.04 seconds of aim, along their nose with twice the normal angular error. Both waste finite ammunition and show their actual miss trajectories. Friendly lanes are checked against the intended aim and dispersed firing endpoints; panic does not authorize friendly fire or let passive target AI shoot. AA endpoints, tracer travel and heavy-burst timing share the solved lead-point flight time and weapon drag. Events expose `aircraft.panic` for review. These behaviors are a gameplay approximation of poor fire discipline, not a historical psychological model.
