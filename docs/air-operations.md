# Carrier aircraft operations

Enterprise's version-1 airWing is authored in its ship blueprint and compiled through the normal ship pipeline. The initial inventory is six Wildcats, six Dauntlesses and six Devastators. Their existing GLBs remain independent asset-pipeline outputs. This reduced mixed group is gameplay tuning.

The Air group instrument launches three available planes per click. Strike aircraft retain the selected hostile surface target. Fighters retain intercept targets, prioritize loaded bombers approaching their carrier, and share targets across the group. They patrol a circuit between the carrier and the selected target when no threat is nearby. Recall cancels queued planes and orders airborne survivors home. Bot carriers launch one group of each role and relaunch surviving aircraft after recovery. Inventory is finite; lost aircraft never respawn during a battle. Returning aircraft rearm and repair in 35 seconds after parking when the service module is available. The flight inspector shows HP, phase, bursts and payloads; the chart shows team-colored aircraft crosses.

The renderer-free fixed tick owns the phase sequence: ready → queued → taxi → takeoff → outbound/attack → returning → landing → rollout → parking → rearming → ready, or lost. Aircraft taxi from stable parking spots to the authored launch datum, accelerate along the deck, and climb away. One aircraft at a time uses the flight lane; the authored three-second minimum also applies, with a faster 22 m/s launch taxi and 3.6-second deck acceleration run. The next taxi starts once the preceding plane has cleared the bow, instead of waiting through its whole climb-out. In the stationary-carrier fixture, the first fighter is airborne in about 12 seconds and a three-plane group in about 36 seconds, versus about 20 and 63 seconds previously. Recovery reserves the lane during an extended final approach as well as rollout and parking. Circuits and landing traffic can add several minutes to a sortie. Returning planes wait for the lane, descend to the actual tyre datum, decelerate for three seconds, and taxi back to their own spots. Turns develop through finite roll rates and bank-driven lateral lift; pitch, throttle, dive speed and pullouts have finite response. These and damage, gun bursts and flight endurance are deterministic gameplay rules. Returning aircraft circle clear of the landing lane, capture the extended deck centerline and account for carrier motion during descent; an unacceptable approach goes around. At most 144 aircraft are airborne across all carriers; queued launches wait for capacity. All 18 surviving aircraft are visible on deck in port and battle when parked, queued or rearming. Deck aircraft follow the displayed hull pose, including roll and pitch. Parking is a compact single row derived from the existing flight-deck datums; aircraft have no deck collision bodies. Aircraft use original engine-shaft origins; tyre clearances are taken from the published deck sockets. Ship roll, pitch, immersion and the service magazine can suspend launches/recovery. Port/reset clears all flights, weapons and losses; pause freezes simulation and articulation.

Fighter bursts require a relative-velocity bullet lead solution, 0.12 seconds of steady aim, a narrow forward gun cone and a clear friendly-aircraft lane within 600 m. Fighters ease their speed in a tail chase and break away from close passes or a fighter on their tail. Supplied, surviving light ship guns provide a bounded AA damage envelope, including on returning flights. AA is abstract and does not train or consume the mounts' surface-gunnery ammunition. Dive bombers stage at separate heights, commit to a descent with dive brakes, and seek a ballistic release solution. Torpedo bombers line up from the nearer beam, settle into a low, level run, and include the payload fall in their intercept. Both can abort an unsafe pass, reposition, and try once more before returning with the payload retained; misses remain misses. Their HE payloads use existing swept ship hits, armor, fragmentation, equipment damage and flood-space rules. Dropped torpedoes fall with gravity, then transition to straight 23 m/s runners at 2 m depth, arm after 180 m and expire at 4.5 km. Friendly hulls can intercept weapons, but do not award hostile damage points. Aircraft strikes attribute ship damage and later frags to the carrier; air kills are separate squadron counters.

The renderer loads three published LODs per supported model, batches each rigid component with instancing, and animates stable propeller, gear, hook, rudder, aileron, elevator and dive-brake IDs. Attitude and mechanism travel interpolate between CPU ticks, including angle wrap; the follow camera samples that same attitude. Gear and hook deploy gradually, ailerons respond to roll input, and split dive brakes open during an actual bombing descent. It displays payloads, falling torpedoes, fighter tracers and brief loss trails; normal combat effects show bomb and torpedo impacts. LOD transitions use 120 and 400 m. When projected wingspan falls below 14 pixels, a small seven-pixel silhouette fades in to prevent thin distant geometry from vanishing between raster samples. It respects scene depth and fog, fades out as binoculars resolve the real model, and is hidden for deck/lost aircraft and inspection. The silhouette uses one bounded instanced draw for the airborne group; it does not change aircraft size or hits in simulation. GPU waves never drive aircraft or weapon outcomes.

Remaining approximations: no full aerodynamic model (including stalls or wind), physical arresting cables, deck collision solver, elevator operations, wing folding, historical air-group manifest, blast damage to aircraft, rescue/diversion, user-piloted cockpit, manual bombing reticle, or historical torpedo failure rates. Fighter ammunition is counted in bursts. Endurance is a time budget. Losing a carrier still participates in ship-based battle victory; airborne survivors cannot resurrect it. See the [Enterprise runtime flight discrepancy register](../assets/ships/enterprise-cv6/reports/flight-discrepancies.md) for tuning and provenance limitations.

AA coverage in this first implementation comes only from registered guns of 40 mm or smaller: Enterprise and Type VIIC have eligible mounts. Bismarck, Yamato and Baltimore currently have no simulated AA envelope; they need friendly carrier fighters for air protection. Adding their historical AA batteries remains future authoring work.

Individual aircraft can be followed from the Air group list, including parked, taxiing and returning planes. The chase camera samples the same displayed hull pose and interpolated airborne position as the renderer. It preserves ship aim, suspends shell follow, and hides the ship reticle while active. Return to ship, camera/recenter controls, port/reset and aircraft loss end tracking; pause holds the flight pose. This is observation, not player flight control.
