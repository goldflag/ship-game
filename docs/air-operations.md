# Carrier aircraft operations

Carriers use the same version-1 `airWing` in their ship blueprints and compile through the normal ship pipeline. Enterprise's finite inventory is **48 aircraft: 16 Wildcats, 16 Dauntlesses and 16 Devastators**, with compatibility six-plane flights and 12 deck positions. Legacy Custom/online limit each carrier to four active flights; desktop PvE has no active-flight cap. All ready aircraft start in the hangar. Their existing GLBs remain independent asset-pipeline outputs. This reduced mixed group is gameplay tuning, not a historical manifest.

Shōkaku's December 1941 preset uses the same global gameplay complement: 48 aircraft, comprising 16 A6M2 Zeros, 16 D3A1 Vals and 16 B5N2 Kates. This deliberately reduced equal-role inventory is not a historical manifest. Each carrier's compatibility six-plane limit produces nine groups (6, 6 and 4 aircraft per role); launch/recovery behavior remains shared, with the concurrent-flight policy chosen by the operating profile. Optional managed four-plane grouping and deck policies are deferred beyond the desktop MVP; see the [PvE implementation status](pve-implementation-status.md). The published Japanese aircraft rigs retain their wings in flight position; A6M2 and B5N2 historical folding hinges are not yet modeled. D3A1 has fixed landing gear and wings. Wildcat and Devastator folding remains driven by their authored hinges, and capability is checked against every exported LOD. The renderer loads the aircraft needed by the selected fleet, with three LODs each, and retains cached models when the fleet changes. See the [Shōkaku reference and discrepancy record](../assets/ships/shokaku/README.md) for configuration uncertainty.

## Rust operating profiles

Rust resolves versioned `AirRules` from the content manifest and freezes the selected values for a battle. `MissionRules.airProfileId` binds a mission to its installed profile; saved PvE setups carry the full selection. Missing profiles, altered selections and a deck allowance above the authored capacity are rejected. Profiles and the mission binding participate in content and simulation hashes.

The explicit compatibility profile is [legacy-air.v1.json](../assets/gameplay/legacy-air.v1.json). It preserves authored group/deck/active-flight values and the existing launch timing, repair ceiling and endurance deadlines. The initial rule contract supports a bounded or unlimited active-flight policy and disabled or timed endurance. Rust admission, automatic recall, exhaustion and recovery priority consume the same endurance policy. Aircraft clocks continue advancing when endurance is disabled; empty weapons and critical damage still cause returns.

Desktop PvE now selects [pve-air.v1.json](../assets/gameplay/pve-air.v1.json). It preserves the compatibility group size, 12 deck positions, 10-second group launch queue and simplified launch/recovery/service cycle, while removing active-flight admission limits. Aircraft inventory and persistent losses remain unchanged: each carrier has nine groups sharing 48 aircraft; unlimited groups never creates airframes or skips deck queues.

The PvE endurance default is **40 minutes of flight time**. New-task admission closes after **30 minutes**; automatic recall starts after **32 minutes** for fighters and bombers, leaving an eight-minute recovery reserve. These are gameplay defaults, not historical fuel-performance claims. Legacy deadlines remain 7:50 for new orders/general recall, 4:20 for fighter recall and 17:30 for exhaustion. Rust admission, general and fighter recall, formation-leader eligibility, exhaustion and recovery priority all use the selected policy; presentation receives that same frozen profile even with a legacy deck cycle. The frozen TypeScript simulation reference retains legacy constants and is not used for active PvE combat.

Empty weapons, critical damage and explicit recall still return aircraft earlier. Search orders remain finite missions: completing a sweep, its distance-derived deadline, two minutes of shadowing, or an observed threat can end the search with a stated notice. Those mission-completion timers do not shorten patrol/defend endurance. Activating a managed physical profile is not required. Further four-plane grouping, 24-plane startup, lift/deck/hangar integration and physical-cycle acceptance remain deferred in the [implementation status](pve-implementation-status.md).

Both carrier definitions now include an optional version-1 `deckLayout`: named parking positions with role preferences, launch and recovery datums, and handling elevators tied to fitted structures. The positions are tyre datums in ship-local metres; they do not override the selected operating policy. Each current layout has 24 positions, with eight preferred for each role. Enterprise uses three columns with its authored wing folds; Shōkaku uses two columns with the current full-span Japanese rigs. Both use the forward elevator for handling; a full-span Kate cannot fit Shōkaku's smaller lifts. These are gameplay handling layouts, not historical deck-spotting plans.

The content build measures parked, spread and intermediate wing-fold bounds and three tyre support points from the published aircraft GLBs. Rust validates and consumes these geometry records without loading renderer assets. Its clearance queries account for translation and intermediate headings, so clear endpoints alone cannot authorize towing through another aircraft. Packing tests establish aircraft-to-aircraft room and a clear forward launch run; the stern recovery paths intersect the full startup arrangement and require clearing. The managed scheduler uses these records for startup, handling and recovery. Aircraft contact fitting is described below; moving platform presentation and integrated startup review remain pending. Numeric capacity and passing bounds tests do not certify complete taxi/recovery clearance.

## Hangar consolidation

`AirRules.consolidation` explicitly selects `hangar-compatible` or `disabled`. Older profiles resolve to the existing hangar-compatible behavior, and the selected value participates in the content handshake. Managed groups consolidate only when every surviving member is in the hangar or already receiving hangar service, their combined count fits the resolved group size, and their models and roles match. Queued or committed handling holds group membership stable until the request finishes or is cancelled; aircraft on deck, transferring or airborne are never moved between groups.

The first group's ID survives. Aircraft retain individual IDs, health, stores, sortie counts and service timers; the removed group remains a merge record with the lost airframes. Friendly escort references and selected group IDs follow the surviving group, including chained merges. The selected command card and air drawer show a merge notice. Addressed launch, service and recall commands using an earlier group ID resolve within that same carrier before normal readiness and ownership checks. Enemy references do not receive private merge information.

Repair below also services healthy aircraft that need ammunition or a payload, without reducing health above the repair ceiling. Existing service timers finish normally after a merge. Native tests cover both carriers and all six current aircraft models; a merged dive-bomber group on each carrier completes repair, lift, launch and recall. Integrated battle and native-input acceptance remain in the implementation tracker.

## PvE observation-based air orders

PvE uses opaque team contact IDs for `Strike` and `InterceptContact`. `Battle.command_air` validates report ownership, hostile affiliation and surface/aircraft kind; normal aircraft admission still checks role, weapons, health and deck readiness. Legacy actor-ID Attack and flight-ID Intercept cannot bypass observations in this mode. Custom/online retain those compatibility commands. The TypeScript presentation type derives from the generated Rust air-order union, with its existing optional Defend field normalization.

Strikes and interceptions navigate from measured position, reported velocity and bounded extrapolation. Only a fresh local aircraft sighting (at most two seconds old) permits attack behavior; receiving another unit's report alone does not. Lost reports lead to a bounded orbit around the estimated search area, with an explicit notice and no hidden homing. Own pilot navigation legs are safe to project to the fleet map. PvE loiter stations use the mission circle with maneuvering space instead of the legacy 30 km carrier-relative limit.

Fighter target selection and evasion use local reports plus owned aircraft. Hostile representations carry measured motion and unknown capability, without inspecting the target's current HP, payload or flight group. Fighter shots resolve against the physical aircraft's predicted position; hitting a report marker cannot damage or overwrite an aircraft that has moved away. This preserves the existing approximate burst model rather than implementing individual bullet trajectories.

Variation D routes aircraft report markers to Intercept and allows strikes against stale surface reports for reacquisition. Mixed selections send the resolved action only to compatible, ready groups and report skipped counts. Actual native-input acceptance remains in the implementation tracker. `SearchArea` adds a finite circular sweep with Low (200 m), Medium (850 m) or High (1,500 m) search altitude. Its radius is 1–6 km in the protocol; the command card offers 2, 4 and 6 km. Admission reserves 1.5 km of maneuvering room inside the battle circle. The Rust route uses alternating chords with altitude-dependent spacing, finite-rate line guidance and a travel-derived deadline. Finishing the route or reaching its deadline returns the aircraft. These are provisional operational values, not historical search patterns.

The three policies are Report only, Shadow and report, and Search and strike. Report-only aircraft keep their weapons and finish the sweep. Shadow acquires a hostile surface report locally, follows its measured movement for at most two minutes and searches the last report if sight is lost. Both scout policies withdraw when a locally observed aircraft closes within 3.5 km or condition falls below 60 HP. Search-and-strike is reserved for armed bombers; it can commit to a locally sighted ship inside the assigned area without a previous scout visit. The existing strike controller continues to require local acquisition. No policy grants hidden target positions or reveals water merely because a route passes nearby.

D exposes these controls on the selected aircraft card. Choose Search area and click water; keyboard users can focus the chart, pan with the arrow keys and press Enter at its center. Aircraft/contact clicks preserve an armed Search order. The chart displays the owned native route and a fading trail of actual five-second position samples from the last 90 seconds. The trail is not an observed swath or a cleared-water guarantee; a sensor-derived swath display remains pending. Search controls stay out of follow/manual-helm views.

The enemy commander runs only for the opposing PvE team. It proposes one carrier action per difficulty cadence: establish CAP, send a bounded rear-area scout without contacts, commit strikes against recent reports and provide fighter escorts. It retains an escort slot under compatibility flight limits. Known identified carrier reports receive priority, without reading hidden composition, damage or position. Managed profiles can request Raise/Repair/Rearm through ordinary deck admission; production still uses the compatibility profile. All air intentions pass the normal authenticated Session handler. This initial doctrine has native information-boundary and allocation tests. The desktop MVP requires competent basic opposition through the selected compatibility profile; complete managed sorties, advanced threat-driven retasking, multi-carrier allocation and exhaustive combat balance are deferred.

## PvE carrier loss and recovery

PvE projects each carrier's recovery state as Open, Delayed or Closed under both compatibility and managed deck profiles. Roll, pitch, sharp turns and temporary service unavailability delay recovery; returning aircraft retain their marshal requests and can land when the deck steadies. Physical carrier loss or destruction of the linked service module closes flight operations and clears managed handling tasks and reservations.

Aircraft aboard a lost carrier are destroyed. Grounded survivors on a carrier with destroyed recovery equipment become unavailable. Airborne survivors may finish a feasible combat order: strikes and interceptions require recent hostile reports, while defense and escort require a surviving friendly assignment. A strike search remains bounded by its search task. Returning aircraft, depleted or critically damaged aircraft, and groups without a feasible task withdraw. No aircraft changes its home carrier. Aircraft alone cannot prevent fleet elimination.

The terminal `withdrawn` state preserves individual IDs, health, ammunition and payload; it emits an `aircraft-unavailable` event once and creates no crash or fabricated combat kill. Telemetry excludes withdrawn aircraft from usable groups, hangar/deck counts and follow targets. The manifest and carrier controls report unavailable aircraft separately from losses and distinguish closed operations from a temporary suspension. These rules apply only to PvE observation-mode battles; legacy Custom/online behavior remains covered by migration comparisons.

## Battlefield camera and squadron commands

The ocean covers the full map view through maximum zoom and panning, including portrait windows. Position and camera tilt ease together in both directions, and reversing a transition starts from the currently displayed pose. The development review at `/scripts/diagnostics/carrier-camera.html` opens the actual renderer at maximum zoom; `carrierCameraReview` exposes frame stepping for inspecting transitions.

**M** switches the existing perspective camera to a high, north-up view of the actual ocean scene, tilted 20° from vertical. The camera rises into the map and descends back to the ship over 1.4 seconds; reduced-motion preferences keep the switch immediate. Map markers and routes track the rendered camera every frame in logical HUD coordinates, so automatic and manual HUD scaling keep them aligned with the scene. Pointer picking and edge panning use viewport pixels. No modal or separate chart replaces it. Ship targets, squadron tags, the selected route and station markers use the same perspective projection as the scene. Route and station-ring segments are clipped to the camera view before perspective division, so targets behind the camera cannot reverse their direction when zooming. Ship nametags use compact 80 × 5 px hull-HP bars with percentages centered below and no vessel-status text. Sunk ships retain only a faded name and cannot receive squadron orders. There is no grid. Command/Ctrl-drag or middle-mouse drag to orbit; Shift+arrow keys also change the angle. Orbit adjusts tilt from overhead to 80° from vertical. **Shift-drag** draws a box around your airborne squadron positions to select them together. **Command/Ctrl-click** a squadron card, map label or manifest row to add or remove it; Command/Ctrl+Shift-drag adds a box to the selection. A normal click selects one squadron, or deselects it when it is the only selection. Orders and Return apply to every selected squadron through the existing role, readiness and flight-slot checks; feedback reports partial acceptance. Selected groups share gold highlights and show their routes. Camera dragging, arrow keys, edge panning, zoom and orbit remain available when **H** hides the instruments. Drag, hold arrow keys, or move the mouse within 32 pixels of a screen edge to pan; scroll to zoom. Camera buttons and the tilt slider are omitted to keep the sea visible. The map zoom radius runs from 300 m to 40 km, allowing twice the previous maximum zoom-in. Zoom preserves the water point under the cursor; near the horizon or over the sky, navigation uses a stable water anchor below the horizon. M or Return to ship restores the ship camera; Esc first cancels a pending target action and otherwise restores the ship camera and its optics; pause, port/reset and following an aircraft retain their existing behavior. Combat continues while overhead, with ship aiming and firing suspended.

The authored squadron pools are divided into stable `flightSize` groups at runtime: both carriers have three groups per role under legacy rules, containing six, six and four aircraft. Their IDs derive from the carrier ID, authored pool ID and group index. After all survivors reach the hangar, depleted groups of the same model and role combine when their combined surviving count fits the authored flight size. The earlier group keeps its ID, the merged group leaves the command roster, and remaining cards show their current numbered shortcuts. Aircraft keep their individual health, service timers, ammunition and IDs; losses never respawn. Compact 90 px cards use baked transparent images of each group’s actual aircraft model, without a repeated selection heading or instructional block. Per-aircraft numbers and follow controls remain available through Aircraft inspection or hover titles. The squadron row appears only in M view; click a box to select it, or use 1–0 for the first ten flights. Flights beyond ten remain selectable in the squadron row and manifest. The wing manifest at the right edge of the M view lists every squadron by role with one cell per aircraft: the cell fills with condition in the aircraft's status color and its underline shows the remaining bomb or torpedo, or the fighter's gun bursts out of `FIGHTER_AMMO_BURSTS`. Its header reports surviving aircraft, active flights against `maxActiveFlights`, deck occupancy against capacity, hangar and recovery-queue counts and a deck-suspended warning; each row shows the squadron's activity, mission and endurance or rearm timer, and clicking a row selects the squadron. The manifest can be hidden from its header, disappears with H, and is omitted at widths of 600 px and below. Clicking the sole selected box or map label, or pressing its number again, deselects it and cancels pending targeting. The normal carrier view retains the gun selection, ammunition and firing controls, with an Air wing card beside ammunition showing the configured M shortcut and opening the map. Number keys continue selecting ship weapons outside M view. Ready, Launching, On mission, Returning, Servicing and Lost remain mutually exclusive aircraft totals.

A selected ready squadron launches with its first valid order. An active squadron receives the same order through the shared CPU validation path:

- Right-click water: all roles fly to and loiter around a station within 30 km of their carrier. Bombers keep their payloads.
- Right-click an enemy ship: armed dive or torpedo bombers strike it.
- Right-click a friendly ship: fighters defend that ship, following its movement.
- Right-click an enemy squadron: fighters intercept that specific squadron.
- Right-click another friendly squadron: fighters escort it.
- Return recalls the selected groups; Recall all cancels queued launches and returns airborne survivors.

In M view, **L** arms loiter, **A** strike, **D** defend, **I** intercept and **E** escort; click a compatible target to issue the order. Fighter/bomber role controls show the available actions and their keys. **R** returns the selected squadrons, **X** recalls all, and **V** toggles individual aircraft inspection. These shortcuts are contextual to the battlefield and leave the normal ship controls available outside it. Esc or pressing the selected action again cancels targeting. Right-click still supplies the contextual target order when no action is pending.

Invalid targets, wrong roles, exhausted armament, servicing squadrons, low endurance and unavailable launch slots reject commands without partially launching or changing the previous order. The selected order survives targeting another ship. Missing defended or escorted targets fall back to carrier defense. A destroyed intercept target becomes a loiter order at the fighter's current area; destroyed ship strike targets return with unused payload.

Name-tag positions and visibility follow interpolated airborne squadron centers and the camera every frame in both views; status text retains the 10 Hz telemetry cadence. Name tags show remaining aircraft and actual activity (Launching, En route, Loitering, Attacking, Engaging, Defending, Intercepting, Returning or Servicing) in the ship and M cameras. Fixed loiter-station crosses and circles exist only in M view; their water positions come from the persistent order, independently of each plane's orbit. Aircraft details retain condition, endurance, recovery queue and follow controls. Lost and hidden hangar aircraft cannot be followed.

## Deck and flight simulation

All ready aircraft start hidden in the hangar. A valid launch queues only the chosen squadron. When released, an aircraft appears at the authored launch datum, deploys any authored folding wings during the 3.6-second run and completes a 2.4-second initial climb, then flies its mission. A shared carrier cadence of `(10 - run - climb) / (flightSize - 1)` spaces departures: a six-plane group completes launch in approximately ten seconds with a clear deck. Overlapping runs are intentional gameplay tuning. A following squadron waits for the launch cadence; aircraft stay hidden while queued. There is no global count cap on aircraft in takeoff or flight. Carrier damage, approaching landings and the authored per-carrier flight/deck rules can suspend launches.

Survivors use the existing downwind, base and final approach circuit with emergency endurance priority, a compressed 60 m separation on final and a 1.2-second deceleration after touchdown. They then transfer into the hangar for the authored 35-second base service interval. Damage extends this by the missing-health fraction: 80 HP takes 42 seconds, 40 HP takes 56 seconds and 10 HP takes 66.5 seconds. Recall during the deck run clears the runway before service. Service repairs survivors up to 60 HP, preserving health already above 60, and restores ammunition and payloads; losses never respawn. The deck/hangar transfer is abstract, without elevators, physical arresting cables or a deck collision solver. These cadence and recovery settings are explicitly gameplay choices.

Bomber transit formations vary deterministically by battle seed, flight and sortie between a V, two three-aircraft sections and echelon. Fighters travel in pairs. Attack runs transition toward trail spacing for dive bombers and line abreast for torpedo bombers; final release corrections remain individual. Stable aircraft slots survive leader changes. Slots transition at up to 8 m/s, with small smooth drift and finite steering/throttle corrections, so changing layout does not teleport an aircraft. Threatened bombers temporarily spread out, then rejoin. Recall uses individual landing circuits.

Bombers react to locally observed fighters approaching within 1.4 km and to actual nearby gunfire. A seeded 4–6-second break changes heading and altitude, followed by a cooldown; wingmen break outward. Healthy aircraft protect a committed dive or low torpedo release window. A critically threatened aircraft below 45 HP can abort that window and retain its payload. Returning bombers can evade outside 4 km of the carrier; the approach, critical damage and low endurance take priority nearer home. Report-only and shadow scouts retain their earlier withdrawal policy. PvE reactions cannot inspect unobserved enemy state. The flight card reports active evasions.

Final recovery guidance follows the translating and rotating deck centerline, including the carrier's yaw velocity, instead of chasing a moving point. Normal turns remain recoverable. Above 0.012 rad/s of carrier yaw, aircraft hold clear and the flight card asks the player to steady the course. Deck-motion and queue delays have separate explanations. A leg with no material progress for 90 seconds restarts the circuit; every second missed final adds a 20-second rejoin interval. These retries retain the recovery request and existing touchdown, separation, deck availability and reservation checks.

Turns develop through finite roll rates and bank-driven lateral lift; pitch, throttle, dive speed and pullouts have finite response. Damage, gun bursts and time-based endurance remain deterministic gameplay rules. The earlier stationary, AA-disabled traffic fixture recovered 24 planes by 663 seconds; those timestamps describe the previous launch/recovery cadence. The current regression still requires all 24 aircraft to recover, rearm and relaunch without endurance losses or exceeding 12 deck aircraft. This does not establish sortie timing under moving-carrier combat; see the [current command validation record](../assets/reviews/carrier-command/README.md) and [earlier traffic measurements](../assets/reviews/carrier-operations/README.md).

Disciplined fighter bursts require a relative-velocity bullet lead solution, 0.12 seconds of steady aim, a narrow forward gun cone and a clear friendly-aircraft lane within 600 m. Fighters use lead pursuit to close, tail and lag pursuit to control closure, and finite break/extend maneuvers to avoid overshoots. A speed advantage can trigger a climbing turn; a prolonged poor firing angle triggers repositioning. A threatened fighter breaks toward a nearby wingman when available, and wingmen prioritize locally observed attackers on allies. Maneuvers have cooldowns and temporarily suspend firing. These tactics still use the shared finite-rate flight model, without a full aerodynamic energy or stall simulation. Supplied, surviving high-angle ship guns fire finite, seeded AA bursts, including at returning flights. Static and moving target AI remains passive. AA shares mount training, ammunition, reload, damage and obstruction rules with surface gunnery and takes priority while an airborne threat is in range. Damage resolves from burst miss distance; it is not a full simulation of each AA projectile or fuze. Dive bombers stage together with a 3 km approach, finish their turn toward the target at altitude, then commit within 1.6 km with dive brakes and seek a ballistic release solution. The approach capture radius accommodates their finite turning radius, including close launches and crossing targets. Torpedo bombers line up from the nearer beam, settle into a low, level run, and include the payload fall in their intercept. Both can abort an unsafe pass, reposition, and try once more before returning with the payload retained; misses remain misses. Their HE payloads use existing swept ship hits, armor, fragmentation, equipment damage and flood-space rules. Dropped torpedoes fall with gravity, then transition to straight runners at 2 m depth and arm after 180 m. The Devastator retains its 23 m/s, 4.5 km gameplay torpedo; the Kate uses a Type 91 Mod 2 at a nominal 42 knots with a 2 km range. The Val carries a Type 99 No. 25 250 kg bomb with a nominal 60 kg filling. Shared arming, contact fuzes, damage and reliability remain game calibration; the Val bomb does not reproduce its historical delayed semi-armor-piercing behavior. Payload selection follows the aircraft model, including in mixed fleets. Friendly hulls can intercept weapons, but do not award hostile damage points. Aircraft strikes attribute ship damage and later frags to the carrier; air kills are separate squadron counters.

The renderer loads three published LODs per supported model, batches each rigid component with instancing, and animates stable wing-fold, propeller, gear, hook, rudder, aileron, elevator and dive-brake IDs. Attitude and mechanism travel interpolate between CPU ticks, including angle wrap; the follow camera samples that same attitude. Gear and hook deploy gradually, ailerons respond to roll input, and split dive brakes open during an actual bombing descent. It displays finned bombs that turn into their falling trajectory, falling torpedoes, and short moving fighter and ship AA tracers. Fighter effects retain the captured gun direction, airframe velocity and attitude; ship AA starts at the actual muzzle and follows its captured launch direction, inherited ship velocity and gravity. Light AA tracers continue for at least three seconds and at least one second beyond the sampled target flight time, with a 0.65-second fade; reaching the sea ends visibility. Ship AA firing snapshots carry the fitted caliber. Tracer cores, envelopes, tips and exposure lengths scale with caliber, so light AA reads as fine, short streaks and heavy AA stays distinguishable. Heavy AA tracers terminate at the CPU-sampled flight time and endpoint, where a brief fireball throws narrow fragments and radial gas fingers. The fragments burn out within 0.38 seconds; the fireball cools within roughly half a second into three overlapping charcoal smoke volumes that expand, drift with wind and disperse over roughly six seconds. The gas fingers disperse within 1.3 seconds. All phases reuse the bounded smoke and ignition pools; late frames sample the burst’s current age without replaying its flash. See the [AA fragmentation and shell-size review](../assets/effects/naval/reports/aa-fragmentation/README.md). Flak remains visible in binoculars, including bursts fired by the player. The renderer retains observed shots independently of the 128-event combat log, and tracer instance storage grows in pages instead of dropping shots at 512. Pause freezes both travel and smoke; reset clears active shots and pending bursts. The development review at `/scripts/diagnostics/anti-aircraft-effects.html` samples travel, burst, drift and expiry on the actual ocean scene. Shot-down aircraft leave combat immediately, then their CPU wreck poses descend under drag and gravity into one sea-impact event. Smoke follows the wreck path and sea impacts generate spray and foam; normal combat effects show bomb and torpedo impacts. LOD transitions use projected wingspan thresholds of 90 and 28 pixels. The lowest-detail model remains rendered below nine pixels rather than being culled by size. A faint, soft contact hint fades in below 12 pixels of projected wingspan to support tiny edge-on geometry. Its size is capped at three pixels and never exceeds the projected wingspan; maximum opacity is 28%. It shrinks with the aircraft and leaves resolved airframes and their paint untouched. The contact has no distance cutoff, respects scene depth and fog, fades out as binoculars resolve the real model, and is hidden for deck/lost aircraft and inspection. Contacts expand into additional small instanced draws as needed; they do not change aircraft size or hits in simulation. GPU waves never drive aircraft or weapon outcomes.

Remaining approximations: wreck descent and tracer spacing are visual/gameplay approximations; wrecks meet the flat gameplay sea without terrain/hull collisions, secondary damage or breakup. Ordnance shapes and wing gun locations are original approximations rather than documented historical reconstructions. No full aerodynamic model (including stalls or wind), physical arresting cables, deck collision solver, elevator operations, historical air-group manifest, blast damage to aircraft, rescue/diversion, user-piloted cockpit, manual bombing reticle, or historical torpedo failure rates. Fighter ammunition is counted in bursts. Endurance is a time budget. Losing a carrier still participates in ship-based battle victory; airborne survivors cannot resurrect it. See the [Enterprise runtime flight discrepancy register](../assets/ships/enterprise-cv6/reports/flight-discrepancies.md) for tuning and provenance limitations.

Registered high-angle guns up to 130 mm now engage hostile airborne aircraft automatically. Bismarck has eight twin 105 mm, eight twin 37 mm and twelve single 20 mm AA mounts connected to its existing original models. Unselected groups operate automatically; a directly selected gun group follows the player’s manual aim and fire commands. Guns train and elevate, fire visible traveling tracers, consume their shared ammunition, and respect damage, magazine supply, obstruction and friendly-lane checks. The two upper quad 20 mm fittings remain decorative. Effective ranges and burst damage are gameplay calibration; heavy AA uses an approximate timed-burst hit radius. Other ships participate according to their registered guns; visual-only fittings do not supply invisible AA damage.

Individual aircraft can be followed from Air operations, including visible parked, taxiing and returning planes. In fleet command, Follow lead works across all friendly carriers, including when the current ship has no aircraft or has sunk. Aircraft follow takes precedence over automatic ship spectating and uses the owning carrier’s deck pose. Ship spectator views retain friendly squadron nametags and enemy aircraft names from that ship’s current sightings. Ready or queued aircraft inside the hangar have a disabled Follow control. The chase camera samples the same displayed hull pose and interpolated airborne position as the renderer. Move the mouse to orbit the followed aircraft; with the cursor released, drag either mouse button or touch instead. Scroll adjusts follow distance from 12–800 m. The same controls work during shell follow. Orbit and zoom preserve the ship’s saved camera, aim and binocular magnification. The camera continues tracking the actual displayed target, clears sea/terrain, and holds its chosen relative offset through translation. Aircraft follow suspends shell follow and hides the ship reticle; mouse clicks operate the follow camera without firing or opening optics. Return to ship, camera/recenter controls, port/reset and aircraft loss end tracking; pause holds the flight pose. This is observation, not player flight control.

Aircraft accuracy uses the battle seed, stable aircraft identity, sortie and attack pass. Fighter bursts sample angular aim error and can miss even after acquiring a firing solution. Strike pilots hold an imperfect aim point for the entire pass: provisional standard deviations are 5 m across the ship and 12 m along it for bombs, 24 m across and 100 m along for torpedoes. Dive guidance compensates for the moving aim point’s angular motion. Pilots require their predicted impact to be within 3 m of that imperfect aim point before release; this is not a guaranteed hit on the ship. These are gameplay settings, not historical hit rates. Bombs inherit actual aircraft velocity and use swept ballistic collision; torpedoes retain their release heading. Different seeds produce both physical hits and misses; identical seeds replay identically. Launching another squadron does not reroll an existing aircraft’s aim.

## Air gunnery tuning and panic

All aircraft retain 100 HP. Successful ship AA checks deal 7 HP at up to 25 mm, 14 HP above 25–80 mm, and 36 HP above 80 mm: respectively fifteen, eight, or three successful hits to destroy a fresh aircraft. Fighter bursts have an 80 HP damage budget, reduced by alignment and range (roughly 15–80 HP per successful burst). Their 16-burst ammunition supply and weapon cooldown are unchanged; pilot discipline adds reacquisition and assessment pauses between firing opportunities. Aircraft return below 25 HP; service repairs them up to 60 HP and preserves health already above that ceiling.

Fighter angular dispersion is `0.026 + 0.018 × |bank|` radians, tightened from `0.070 + 0.040 × |bank|`; panic still doubles dispersion. Fighter damage, range and ammunition remain unchanged. Ship AA per-hit damage is reduced by approximately 10–12%, retaining its range, cadence, mount mechanics and finite ammunition. Tuning lives in `crates/naval-sim/src/air_gunnery.rs`; the TypeScript controller is a frozen migration reference.

Deterministic observed-mode engagements in `crates/naval-sim/tests/air_balance.rs` cover viable unopposed strikes, positioned fighter interception, mixed high/low raids, mounted AA and combined defense. They isolate the initially assigned fighter group so automatic relief cannot inflate its results. These are gameplay calibration cases with stationary ships and payload impacts excluded, not historical performance claims. Interception geometry and timing matter: head-on kills may occur after release. AA remains complementary; changed evasive/return trajectories can increase exposure and total AA kills despite reduced per-hit damage.

Automatic AA crews and fighter pilots can enter 2–4-second panic episodes while engaging hostile aircraft, followed by 4–7 seconds of recovery before another episode can begin. At each decision, the provisional chance rises from 18% to 60% with pressure: damaged mounts/pilots and close inbound threats to AA or nearby fighter targets. Episodes use independent crew/aircraft identity and the battle seed, persist in serializable CPU state, and stop when contact is lost.

Panicked AA crews discard their velocity lead and train toward a wrong patch of sky, offset 10–34 degrees horizontally and up to about 21 degrees vertically, bounded above the horizon. Mount traverse, elevation, obstruction, reload and ammunition rules still apply. Panicked fighter pilots retain twice the normal angular error, but now require a closer, settled solution and pause after each burst instead of exhausting their ammunition in a moment. Both waste finite ammunition and show their actual miss trajectories. Friendly lanes are checked against the intended aim and dispersed firing endpoints; panic does not authorize friendly fire or let passive target AI shoot. AA endpoints, tracer travel and heavy-burst timing share the solved lead-point flight time and weapon drag. Events expose `aircraft.panic` for review. These behaviors are a gameplay approximation of poor fire discipline, not a historical psychological model.


### Decision cadence

Deciding *what* to shoot at is slower than shooting. A fighter pilot re-runs the flight's track allocation only when the 0.65-second think timer expires, and a ship's AA mount looks for a nearer aircraft only every sixth tick (0.1 s), staggered across a battery by a hash of the mount id. Everything else stays at 60 Hz: flight integration, gun lead and release, evasion and break reactions, formation and lane keeping, ammunition, reload and damage all run every tick against the held track. A track that dies, leaves range, leaves visibility or turns away from the defended station is dropped on the spot, and the search runs again on that same tick.

Both cadences key off simulated time, so 1× and 4× and the multiplayer server stay identical. Neither is stored in a snapshot or a save: a held track is a cadence, not authority state. The effects are small but real — a fighter can stay with a track for up to 0.65 s after a better one appears, and an AA mount can keep firing at a plane for up to 0.1 s after a closer one crosses its sights. The `crates/naval-sim/tests/air_balance.rs` and `air_strike_balance.rs` thresholds are calibrated against this cadence.

### Coordinated fighter defense

Fighters in the same flight allocate observed tracks deterministically. Pairs divide high and low threats when both are present, assign separate targets before doubling up, and prioritize inbound motion relative to the defended area. Idle area cover separates pairs vertically. Defend and Patrol release departing tracks beyond 3.5 km and keep engagements within 7.5 km of the station; explicit interception retains its contact filter and escort follows its friendly anchor. Separate flights retain their own orders.

Calm pilots require a settled, close firing solution and reacquire after each burst. A short two-burst opportunity is followed by an assessment pause; panic uses a stricter, single-burst opportunity. Both intended and dispersed firing lanes remain checked for friendlies. Wingman support and finite-rate lead, lag, break, extension and vertical maneuvers remain part of the fighter controller.

### Continuous patrol relief

An accepted fighter Defend or Patrol order establishes a persistent station. Its original group and at most one automatically chosen relief group rotate through the real launch, recovery and service cycle. Low ammunition, damage, losses or approaching endurance deadlines request relief; the outgoing group is recalled once relief reaches the station. A closed deck or unavailable airframes can still produce a coverage gap, with a flight notice explaining the wait.

The scheduler preserves one untasked fighter group in reserve and does not take aircraft assigned to strikes, interception or escort. Parked default orders do not start patrols. Recalling or retasking either rotation member cancels the station. Inventory, losses, ammunition, service, deck admission and active-flight limits remain authoritative. Managed profiles request normal deck handling; the compatibility cycle remains the production default.

### Coordinated bomber packages

Up to three bomber groups from one carrier, ordered against the same ship/contact within 30 seconds, form a package. Locally acquired target solutions guide complementary attack bearings and a bounded rendezvous: at most 20 seconds, with a six-second dive-bomber offset when torpedo aircraft are present. Single groups proceed without waiting. A shared 25-second window after the first release ends straggling attack passes; surviving aircraft leave along a bounded outward leg before normal recovery. Critical damage and endurance reserve take priority. These are small, carrier-local packages, without cross-carrier orchestration or guaranteed simultaneous impacts.

### Aircraft performance

[aircraft-performance.v1.json](../assets/gameplay/aircraft-performance.v1.json) defines the six installed models' gameplay speed, acceleration, roll, bank, climb and turn-drag profiles, with role defaults for unknown models. The file participates in the simulation hash. Zeros accelerate and climb faster and sustain tighter turns than Wildcats; Wildcats reverse bank faster. Vals are more agile than Dauntlesses, while Kates have more speed and climb than Devastators. Damage and carried weapons reduce performance, climbing and turning consume energy, and descents recover speed within the model's limit. Launch and landing retain prescribed carrier-relative speeds and recovery constraints. These are readable gameplay differences, not a validated historical aerodynamic model.

### Bomb and aerial torpedo balance

Torpedo approach guidance includes the remaining aircraft leg, water-entry delay and torpedo run when leading the observed ship motion. This produces a stable approach against a steady moving target while preserving bank, pitch, altitude, alignment, range and friendly-lane release gates. Once released, torpedoes retain their heading: they do not home, and a subsequent course change can defeat the solution.

Aerial torpedo nominal damage is 160, reduced from 480 for both installed weapon families. The native unprotected direct-hit hull budget is 3,500 HP before target limits; fitted torpedo protection still reduces it. Breach and flooding remain meaningful, and ship-launched torpedoes retain their existing tuning. A six-plane aerial strike is dangerous but its direct hits cannot nearly erase a healthy carrier or battleship's hull in one wave.

Bombs retain their existing nominal explosive charge and fragment penetration. A direct bomb contact can now spread a bounded share of blast damage into nearby hull regions instead of exhausting only a small flight-deck or upperworks region. Each structural ray respects intervening protection, shielding attenuation and radial falloff; regional saturation and the per-weapon hull budget remain. This extra structural propagation applies only to bombs contacting the ship. Native regression tests cover small-target accuracy, moving-target torpedo releases, repeated local bomb damage, shielding, ordinary HE isolation and capital-ship torpedo survival.


### Physical ground routing

`deck_navigation.rs` checks measured aircraft envelopes against tyre support, authored structural volumes and other aircraft/reservations. Fixed obstacles use triangle-clipped height bands; wings may overhang the deck while all three tyres remain supported. `RouteSearch` preserves progress between ticks and owns its aircraft geometry. Its caller must increment the deck revision when occupancy, reservations, folding or ship geometry changes; a mismatched revision invalidates even a completed route. Use a bounded `advance` call in simulation. The blocking `route` helper is for offline layout tests only. This groundwork does not yet replace the legacy operating cycle.

### Fitted tyre contact

The content build also extracts the lower 15 cm of each deployed tyre assembly as aircraft-local triangles. Rust shares these immutable patches between route searches and compiles the authored flight-deck surface and elevator tops into a bounded spatial cache. It solves root height, pitch and roll against the three tyre patches using triangle-interior overlap, including crowned decks and platform edges. Unsupported positions fail placement. A bounded root adjustment of up to 2.5 cm can settle edge tangencies; the routing datum stays fixed. This is a rigid resting-pose approximation, with no tyre deformation or cable dynamics.

`deckDatum` records the route position separately from the fitted `deckPosition`. Parked aircraft reuse their local fit while their world pose follows current CPU carrier motion; restoring serialized state recomputes that cache. The renderer and follow camera compose the fitted local attitude with the displayed hull pose. Failed movement preserves the last supported datum and handling progress. A failed first lift placement retains its route search for retry, and a failed takeoff step follows the moving hull without repeatedly emitting launch events.

The exact-GLB contact check in the [aircraft pipeline](aircraft-pipeline.md) compares native poses against all three published LODs and separates structural support from paint and other fittings. Moving elevator platforms, their empty cycles and integrated physical review belong to future managed-profile acceptance. They are deferred and do not gate the compatibility-based desktop MVP.

### Arrestor-hook clearance

The aircraft content build measures a `hookDeckFraction` stop from each published rig. Managed deck placement clamps current and previous hook controls to that stop, including the first touchdown tick, so interpolation cannot drive the shoe through the nominal tyre plane. The baked sweep includes the allowed hook travel. The original hook and tail-gear meshes retain their contact geometry at every LOD; the shared fork, hinge and shoe remain an explicit mechanical approximation.

The stop includes a 4 cm nominal allowance. Tyre fitting handles the authored deck shape separately; the stop does not simulate arresting cables or provide moving elevator platforms. Use the [aircraft pipeline clearance check](aircraft-pipeline.md) after hook or gear edits; it samples independently moving hook/gear triangles against exact published hashes.

### Managed deck cycle

`AirRules.deckCycle` selects the compatibility or managed cycle. Desktop PvE binds the compatibility profile as its accepted MVP behavior; the optional managed cycle described here remains deferred pending its own integration and physical review. `deck_operations.rs` and `deck_recovery.rs` manage requests, timed movement and runway/parking reservations against the existing aircraft inventory. Raise and recovery use opposite parking orders because they enter from opposite ends of the deck. Normal service/handling requests reference stable groups; early recovered members can be stowed without waiting for their airborne group mates. `AirWingState.deck` exposes queue, task, suspension, capacity, resolved group size, active-flight allowance, endurance policy and repair ceiling to owned presentation. Automatic clearance tasks are distinguished from user requests; only user requests can be cancelled or selected as the next task. The default Balanced policy alternates bounded aircraft batches when both operations are feasible. Launch first and Recover first double the respective aircraft allowance; changing policy retains completed batch counts. Physical clearance and committed moves always take precedence.

Managed ground phases are `hangar`, `raising`, `taxi`, `ready`, `queued`, `launch-ready`, `lowering`, `repairing`, `rollout`, `parking` and `rearming`. Aircraft retain their IDs and damage through transfers. `Aviation.deck_command` handles service requests; a launch must use a validated flight order. Variation D offers Bring up, Send below, Rearm, Repair below and per-carrier queue cancellation through addressed Rust commands. Split-group service instructions persist for later arrivals; landed members can move immediately. Damaged aircraft already in the hangar can receive Repair without being raised first. Service countdowns use native aircraft timers; the current lift step exposes its remaining duration. Clearance/path waits remain explicit without a fabricated ETA. The client reads authoritative managed group records and does not reconstruct groups from slot indices. Make next is a soft preference after committed moves and required clearance. Unsafe full-deck or conflicting lift priorities are rejected with an explanation; pending tasks remain skippable if conditions change. Elevator visuals and final fitted-ground review remain pending; see the implementation-status checkpoint for the exact test scope.

One handling job runs at a time. A lift may begin during the final takeoff roll in a queued batch once its top position and taxi route clear the departing aircraft's entire remaining runway travel. Earlier queued takeoffs retain priority; incoming recoveries still prevent a new lift. Route searches stay bounded per tick and remain valid as the takeoff reservation shrinks. The departing aircraft keeps its parking/capacity reservation until leaving the runway. If it still owns the preferred aft parking spot, the lift waits for that spot rather than filling a forward hole that would obstruct later arrivals. Complex route searches or a crowded deck can therefore defer the lift until after takeoff; concurrency is conditional on physical clearance, not a fixed timing promise.

## PvE reconnaissance reports

The Surface search layer records sampled visual coverage from surviving friendly ships and airborne aircraft. Every five seconds, Rust checks the center and corners of 1 km cells against the same range, weather, horizon and baked-terrain visibility used by lookouts. The reference target is a 100 m surface vessel with a 5 m visible feature. Shading distinguishes current surveys, the past minute and older history; pointing at water shows the recorded survey age. This is neither an aircraft trail nor a guarantee of empty water: smaller vessels, submerged targets and aircraft have different signatures, and terrain between samples may conceal them. Coverage is bounded to 4,096 cells per team, with at most 64 actual observer representatives and 65,536 probes per survey. Adjacent cells of the same age category become shared display strips.

PvE uses the binary `visual-sensors.v2.json` profile. Range, horizon, weather and terrain determine visibility, with no identification delay or stronger model-visibility threshold. Visible ship and aircraft exterior poses refresh every six simulation ticks (10 Hz). The renderer smooths those poses and keeps big-map markers attached to their displayed models; a ship camera requires its own lookout. Only exterior appearance is transmitted for enemy aircraft, without their carrier, orders, health or inventory.

Current contacts retain exact observed positions and motion, without coordinate rounding. Once observation stops, moving tracks show a separate estimate from their last measured course and speed; stale contacts use their last measured position. Fire, heavy smoke and a pronounced list are reported only from visual observations of exterior cues, with their own observation time. Internal HP, ammunition, machinery, inventory and orders remain private. A sinking is confirmed only through a permitted observation or a visible sinking event; that confirmation remains in the report and removes the contact from legal attack targets. Sinking hulls cannot continue observing for their former team. Losing an observer never refreshes old coverage or reveals subsequent hidden movement or damage. Existing full-knowledge Custom/online projections do not gain this reconnaissance data.

Aircraft map markers and enemy group labels project the same interpolated or smoothed positions as the aircraft on every rendered frame; telemetry updates only their status text and membership. Aircraft status text follows current flight/deck phases. Sortie notices such as scout withdrawal appear only while aircraft remain airborne or the group is launching; after the last landing, servicing, hangar and readiness status replace the old mission text. Patrol groups distinguish launching, en route, loitering and returning.
