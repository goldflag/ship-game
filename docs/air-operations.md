# Carrier aircraft operations

Enterprise's version-1 `airWing` is authored in its ship blueprint and compiled through the normal ship pipeline. Its finite inventory is **48 aircraft: 18 Wildcats, 18 Dauntlesses and 12 Devastators**, with six-plane flights, four active flights and 12 deck positions. The remaining ready aircraft start in the hangar. Their existing GLBs remain independent asset-pipeline outputs. This reduced mixed group is gameplay tuning, not a historical manifest.

## Chart, roster and commands

Press **M** to open Air operations, releasing mouse aim while combat continues. M, Esc or Back to sea closes it; firing is suppressed while it is open. Drag or use arrow keys to pan, scroll or use −/+ to zoom, and choose Fit air wing or Center carrier. The chart displays coasts, ship headings, friendly and hostile aircraft, selectable flight markers and individual aircraft at closer zoom. The selected flight's dashed route combines its lead aircraft's current CPU navigation point with the mission destination. Approximate flight time excludes attack maneuvers, landing waits, go-arounds and service.

Ready, Launching, On mission, Returning, Servicing and Lost are mutually exclusive totals that account for every aircraft. Select a total to inspect its aircraft. Ready room exposes available aircraft by squadron, deck/hangar occupancy and six-plane launch buttons; the flight roster links to chart selection. Flight details expose the persistent order, condition, ammunition or payload count, lowest endurance, recovery queue position, service timer and individual aircraft follow controls. A compact air-wing summary remains available when the chart is closed.

Selecting a hostile ship sets the target for **new** bomber flights. **Assign strike target** redirects the selected bomber flight by selecting an enemy ship on the chart. Fighters support **Defend carrier**, **Patrol area** (select a point), and **Escort flight** (select another friendly flight). CPU validation rejects incompatible roles, exhausted armament, invalid or friendly strike targets, self-escort and patrol points beyond 30 km. Each flight retains its order when the ship target changes. Destroyed strike targets trigger return with unused payload; an unavailable escort group falls back to carrier defense. **Return flight** recalls only that flight; **Recall all flights** cancels all queued launches and orders airborne survivors home. A flight occupies its slot through launch, mission and return until all survivors have parked for service or its queued launch is canceled. The subsequent rearming timer does not occupy a flight slot.

Bot carriers launch their role groups and relaunch survivors after recovery. Lost aircraft never respawn during a battle. Parked survivors rearm and repair for 35 seconds when the service module is available. Port/reset clears flights, weapons and losses; pause freezes simulation and articulation.

## Deck and flight simulation

The renderer-free fixed tick owns the phase sequence: ready → queued → taxi → takeoff → outbound/attack → returning → landing → rollout → parking → rearming → ready, or lost. A deck slot is separate from phase: ready or queued planes may be in the hangar. Abstract four-second transfers spot queued aircraft in a free position, moving an idle ready aircraft below if needed. Twelve stable parking positions bound Enterprise's visible deck population. Hangar planes are neither drawn nor followable. There is no elevator animation or physical deck collision solver.

Aircraft taxi at a tuned 35 m/s onto the authored launch datum, accelerate through a 3.6-second deck run and climb away. One aircraft at a time uses the launch lane; the next taxi can start after the preceding plane clears the bow. At most 144 aircraft are airborne across all carriers; additional launches remain queued. The renderer allocates separately for each carrier's authored deck capacity and the shared airborne cap.

Returning aircraft use downwind, base and final approach legs, with staggered holding heights and persistent circuit side. Recovery prioritizes low endurance among aircraft aligned to capture final, maintains at least 260 m of separation and matches final speed to the landing aircraft to prevent catch-up. The deck is reserved near touchdown and during rollout, followed by a lateral move clear of the runway before parking. An unacceptable or blocked approach goes around. Ship movement is included in approach and descent guidance. Deck aircraft sample the displayed hull roll, pitch and position; original engine-shaft origins and published tyre-clearance sockets remain authoritative. Ship list, pitch, immersion and service-module failure can suspend deck operations.

Turns develop through finite roll rates and bank-driven lateral lift; pitch, throttle, dive speed and pullouts have finite response. Damage, gun bursts and time-based endurance remain deterministic gameplay rules. A stationary, AA-disabled 24-aircraft traffic fixture recovered every launched plane by 663 seconds with no endurance losses and no more than 12 deck aircraft. This does not establish sortie timing under moving-carrier combat; see the [carrier-operations validation record](../assets/reviews/carrier-operations/README.md).

Fighter bursts require a relative-velocity bullet lead solution, 0.12 seconds of steady aim, a narrow forward gun cone and a clear friendly-aircraft lane within 600 m. Fighters ease their speed in a tail chase and break away from close passes or a fighter on their tail. Supplied, surviving light ship guns provide a bounded AA damage envelope, including on returning flights. AA is abstract and does not train or consume the mounts' surface-gunnery ammunition. Dive bombers stage at separate heights, commit to a descent with dive brakes, and seek a ballistic release solution. Torpedo bombers line up from the nearer beam, settle into a low, level run, and include the payload fall in their intercept. Both can abort an unsafe pass, reposition, and try once more before returning with the payload retained; misses remain misses. Their HE payloads use existing swept ship hits, armor, fragmentation, equipment damage and flood-space rules. Dropped torpedoes fall with gravity, then transition to straight 23 m/s runners at 2 m depth, arm after 180 m and expire at 4.5 km. Friendly hulls can intercept weapons, but do not award hostile damage points. Aircraft strikes attribute ship damage and later frags to the carrier; air kills are separate squadron counters.

The renderer loads three published LODs per supported model, batches each rigid component with instancing, and animates stable propeller, gear, hook, rudder, aileron, elevator and dive-brake IDs. Attitude and mechanism travel interpolate between CPU ticks, including angle wrap; the follow camera samples that same attitude. Gear and hook deploy gradually, ailerons respond to roll input, and split dive brakes open during an actual bombing descent. It displays payloads, falling torpedoes, fighter tracers and brief loss trails; normal combat effects show bomb and torpedo impacts. LOD transitions use 120 and 400 m. When projected wingspan falls below 14 pixels, a small seven-pixel silhouette fades in to prevent thin distant geometry from vanishing between raster samples. It respects scene depth and fog, fades out as binoculars resolve the real model, and is hidden for deck/lost aircraft and inspection. The silhouette uses one bounded instanced draw for the airborne group; it does not change aircraft size or hits in simulation. GPU waves never drive aircraft or weapon outcomes.

Remaining approximations: no full aerodynamic model (including stalls or wind), physical arresting cables, deck collision solver, elevator operations, wing folding, historical air-group manifest, blast damage to aircraft, rescue/diversion, user-piloted cockpit, manual bombing reticle, or historical torpedo failure rates. Fighter ammunition is counted in bursts. Endurance is a time budget. Losing a carrier still participates in ship-based battle victory; airborne survivors cannot resurrect it. See the [Enterprise runtime flight discrepancy register](../assets/ships/enterprise-cv6/reports/flight-discrepancies.md) for tuning and provenance limitations.

AA coverage in this first implementation comes only from registered guns of 40 mm or smaller: Enterprise and Type VIIC have eligible mounts. Bismarck, Yamato and Baltimore currently have no simulated AA envelope; they need friendly carrier fighters for air protection. Adding their historical AA batteries remains future authoring work.

Individual aircraft can be followed from Air operations, including visible parked, taxiing and returning planes. Ready or queued aircraft inside the hangar have a disabled Follow control. The chase camera samples the same displayed hull pose and interpolated airborne position as the renderer. It preserves ship aim, suspends shell follow, and hides the ship reticle while active. Return to ship, camera/recenter controls, port/reset and aircraft loss end tracking; pause holds the flight pose. This is observation, not player flight control.
