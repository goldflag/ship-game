# Player guide

How the game plays: port, battles, controls, fleet command and carrier operations. Setup, architecture and
the model pipeline are in the [README](../README.md).

**Battle** in port opens one full-screen board for every mode, with **Custom battle**, **Fleet command** and **1v1 online** as tabs; the button remembers the last mode and its caret opens a mode menu. The ship catalog on the left is shared: search, class and nation chips, and one card per hull showing its silhouette, class icon and detailed type, nation flag, tonnage and, for carriers, embarked aircraft. Ships are placed by drag and drop into the lanes in the middle, or by choosing a card and then a lane for keyboard use. Research decides which historical classes sail in your own fleet: the command berth, the friendly lane, fleet command task groups and 1v1 berths take only the classes you have unlocked in the tech tree and your own designs. A locked card carries a padlock and **Unlock in the tech tree**; Valiant, Resolute and the merchant ships are marked **Enemy only**. The enemy lane takes any ship, so a locked card can still be dragged there. A remembered or carried-over fleet drops the ships it may not bring, a command berth left empty takes an unlocked ship, and the status line says what changed. While research progress is loading, or when it cannot load, every class in the trees stays open. Custom battle has a **Friendly** lane, whose first berth is the ship you command, and an **Enemy** lane; dropping a catalog ship on the command berth takes the helm, and bots can be dragged between lanes. Each bot’s AI level is a compact select in its chip, and **All bots** in a lane header sets every bot on that team at once. The settings rail on the right selects **Waters** (North Atlantic, Pacific Islands, Arctic Passage, or Volcanic Coast in the Indian Ocean) from map tiles, then **Conditions**: time of day (24 hours in 15-minute steps), cloud cover (0–100%) and wind speed (0–30 m/s in 0.5 m/s steps). Defaults are noon, 38% cloud cover and 9 m/s wind. Time stays fixed throughout the battle. Cloud cover and wind are independent; wind drives waves, cloud and smoke drift, and CPU ship motion. **Deployment** holds the spawn distance slider and formation preset. Choices are retained for the current page session, and a fleet carries into another mode’s tab when that mode is still empty, as far as its rules allow. Map haze remains map-specific; bot targeting and ballistics are unchanged. Coastal maps have original procedural islands shown on the navigation chart. Land blocks ships and low projectiles; bots turn away from shores. Large fleets widen the clear deployment lane automatically. Returning to port restores the harbor's daylight and its standing 9 m/s wind, whose sea is the same calibrated one a battle resolves; the ship on show rides it at its berth. See the map guide with in-game screenshots.

You start in your home port, which berths the historical ships you own and the ships you have designed: the ship you last berthed lies alongside, else Cleveland. Every player owns the starters (Gleaves, Cleveland, Fubuki, Mogami, Admiral Hipper, Type VIIC and Flower). The port camera handles like the shipbuilder's and has no fences: drag to orbit, including down through the surface to look up at the hull, right-drag (or Shift-drag) to slide the view anywhere, underwater too, and scroll to zoom from a fitting's length away to the whole ship at a distance. Double-click, or the recenter key, always brings the opening view back. Choose **Battle** to configure both fleets. Choose your own ship, add up to 29 friendly bots, and select one to 30 enemy bots, for up to 30 ships per side. Any registered preset can sail on the enemy team, including duplicates; your own side takes the classes you have unlocked and your designs. **Spawn distance** sets the separation between formations from 1–20 km in 0.5 km steps (5 km by default). **Deploy fleet** opens the deployment chart with both fleets: each formation is drawn as a frame with a name tag, and the **Group / Ship** scope (G and S) decides whether selecting a ship selects its whole formation or just that ship. Drag a frame or its tag to move a formation, drag a ship marker to move one ship, and drag the compass ring around the selection to rotate it in 5° steps (15° with Shift). The heading dial and field, ↶/↷ 15° buttons, arrow keys (500 m, or 100 m with Shift), Q and E (15°), scroll to zoom, drag to pan, Home, Undo and **Reset positions** are also available; roster rows drag onto the chart too. **Line abreast**, **Column**, or **Wedge** arrange both fleets with 650 m spacing. The chart shows the selected map’s coastlines and reports positions near land or within 350 m of another ship, which disable **Start battle** until fixed. Changing formation or spawn distance resets custom positions; removing ships preserves the remaining placements. **Start battle** loads the chosen models at the plotted positions and headings, which also survive battle resets. **Return to port** in the pause menu ends the battle and resets every ship. The port keeps a transparent top bar and hangs the ship's builder's plate (name, principal dimensions, speed, main battery, **Edit design** and **Delete**) at lower left, with the five statistics scores and the Statistics / Armor / Internals / Flooding views at lower right. The side arrows, the ← and → keys and the thumbnail fleet line beneath the ship step through your fleet in place, owned ships grouped by nation and then your ready designs; the harbor stays loaded, your orbit direction is preserved, and camera distance adjusts to keep the same relative zoom for the new hull. **All designs** opens the plan chest: every saved design, drafts included, as large plates with search, Ready for sea / Drafts filters and Recent / Name / Size sorting. **Tech tree**, beside **Battle**, shows the berthed ship's nation XP and your free XP (hover it for every nation) and opens the research tree over the quay: a tab per nation, a column per line (destroyers, cruisers, battleships, carriers, and German submarines or British escorts) with its classes oldest first. Owned ships carry a check; a ship within reach says where the XP comes from (her nation's XP first, then free XP) and unlocks with **Unlock** and then **Confirm**, which cannot be undone; one out of reach says how much XP is missing or which ship must come first; dashed classes are not in the game yet. **View in port** berths any modelled ship, locked or not, to orbit and inspect; while a locked ship is alongside, **Battle** becomes **Unlock · cost**, disabled with the reason until you can have her. If research progress cannot load, every ship stays open and unlocking waits. The fictional Valiant and Resolute and the merchant ships are enemy-only and never join your fleet; the [preset registry](../src/ships/presets.ts) is still the authoritative roster, and a `?ship=<preset-id>` link keeps any preset alongside for review. Currency, refits, and commanders are illustrative, with temporary state only.

Custom battles end when either side has no physically surviving vessels or after **30 minutes of unpaused simulation time**. At the limit, the side with the most original displacement afloat wins; equal tonnage or simultaneous destruction is a draw. Each ship contributes its full catalog mass rounded to the nearest kilogram until irreversible hull loss, flooding or capsize. Damage, disarmament and intentional submarine depth do not reduce that score. The battle HUD's upper-left tally displays time remaining, a hull gauge for every ship on both sides and both fleets' afloat tonnage; holding Ctrl unfolds the roster, where an enemy row designates your target. Press **M** to order the rest of your fleet from the fleet chart without leaving your ship: it keeps its last engine and rudder orders until you return with M or **Return to helm** (a carrier keeps M for Air operations and opens the chart from the tally's **Fleet chart** command). Results lock at the end of the deciding tick. The battle plays on for five more seconds with the HUD up, then the end screen opens over the sea while the camera circles your ship: the result, your research XP and how far it took you toward the next ship you can research, your ship's numbers and both fleets' fate, with **Battle again**, **New battle**, **Return to port** and **Full report**. The full report has four tabs: **Your battle** (your ship drawn with its hits, what hurt it most and how much of each enemy's damage was yours), **Fleets** (both fleets in one table, damage over the battle and who hit whom), **Battle plot** (every ship's hits over time) and **Hits** (any ship's hits on its 3D model). The end screen waits for you. An interrupted or abandoned battle says so at once and returns to port after a 15-second countdown in real time.

Every decided battle, whether custom, fleet command or 1v1, earns research XP once. The end screen shows it under the result as **+N XP** with its split between the nation trees your fleet sailed for and free XP (for example *United States 338 · Free 38*), and a bar toward the next ship you can research, in the line you sailed when it has one; player designs and a tenth of every award go to free XP. The award waits a few seconds for shells still in flight to land, then saves; leaving the report sooner saves it at once. A failed save shows **Retry** on the same line and leaves the rest of the report alone. Sinking and damaging enemies, battle time and the odds you faced add to it, and a victory pays most; static and moving targets are worth nothing. Sea trials, interrupted and abandoned battles earn nothing, and **Battle again** is a new battle that earns again.

Each friendly and enemy bot has its own **AI level** in the custom battle roster, including duplicate ships: **1 · Static target**, **2 · Moving target**, **3 · Easy**, **4 · Normal**, or **5 · Hard**. Normal is the default and retains the existing combat behavior. Static targets issue no movement orders; moving targets turn across the deployment lane and sail a steady course, steering clear of nearby ships and shores. Both target modes keep their weapons silent, including torpedoes, depth charges, carrier aircraft and anti-aircraft defenses. They still take damage, collide, flood and sink. Easy crews acquire targets slowly and fire less accurately; Hard crews track faster, aim more accurately and react sooner to damage. Skill changes crew decisions and carrier launch timing, while ship health, weapon performance and aircraft pilots use the same rules. Choices remain with each roster entry during the page session and across battle resets; your own ship stays under manual control.

Each preset's battle-setup card uses a baked image of its actual model. `bun run ship:thumbnail <ship-id>` regenerates it with local Blender; `ship:build` also refreshes it. Custom ships bake a transparent image of their compiled hull, paint and fittings in the browser for the port's fleet line and plan chest and for battle setup. Images are cached by content hash across reloads and refreshed after design changes.

Ships fly period national ensigns with cloth that responds to weather wind, forward/reverse speed and turning. A coarse cloth grid runs at 60 Hz to limit fleet animation cost. Calm flags hang under gravity; stronger wind stretches and flutters the fabric. Search aerials rotate, and director-mounted radars sweep their authored sectors. Flags animate in port and battle; radars stay stationary in port and animate at sea. Both hold on pause and reset with the ship. Flags hide when their hoists submerge; radar motion stops on sinking. Flag positions, radar rates and sector motion are visual approximations documented in the rig review.

Port camera distance, aim point, height offsets and zoom limits scale with the selected hull's length, including small boats. Switching ships preserves your orbit direction and relative zoom; water and terrain clearance still apply.

Use **Exterior / Armor / Internals** above the port's detail panel to inspect the loaded ship. Armor shows hull and gunhouse protection with thicknesses and material/provenance where recorded. Plates and list swatches share a fixed thickness scale: green at 0 mm, yellow at 200 mm, and red at 400 mm and above; teak backing is gray. Port armor is opaque over the faded actual ship model, with soft directional shading to distinguish slopes and plate edges and an outline only on the hovered plate. Hover a visible plate to highlight it and read its name, thickness, material, dimensions and recorded basis. Internals shows machinery, magazines, steering and flooding compartments. Filter the list and select a row to isolate that space against the ghosted exterior and read its dimensions; this also reveals armor layers hidden behind outer plates. **Clear selection** restores the complete layout. Drag/scroll still orbits and zooms; **Exterior** or Esc returns to the normal model. These are the same provisional volumes used by combat. Inspection does not advance combat or modify the ship.


## Controls

In port, upward dragging stops at the lowest orbit and keeps the camera aimed at the ship. While sailing, upward tilt is limited to 30°. Surface-ship cameras maintain at least 12 m of clearance above sea level, including during sinking; the port also preserves clearance above terrain. Submarine chase follows the hull underwater. Bridge and binocular views use the raised periscope when submerged; at 7 m its eye remains above the sea. Tactical stays above the water.

| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| W / S or up / down                | Raise / lower engine order; tap for each notch                               |
| A / D or left / right             | Step the persistent rudder order toward port / starboard; tap for each notch |
| Space                             | Stop engine; the ship coasts down                                            |
| U / J                             | Submarine: order Surface / 50 m depth                                       |
| Z / X                             | Submarine: order 2 m deeper / shallower                                     |
| P                                 | Submarine: raise / lower periscope view at the current depth                  |
| B                                 | Submarine: emergency blow ballast and surface                                |
| Mouse                             | Aim the centered sight while sailing; drag to orbit in port or inspection    |
| Shift                             | Toggle binocular aiming                                                      |
| Hold right mouse                  | Lock the aim: guns hold their point while you look around; release to return |
| Scroll                            | Adjust camera distance, or continuous 1×–32× binocular magnification         |
| G / L                             | Measure a ship’s range / lock or unlock scope elevation                     |
| Hold Ctrl                         | Release cursor to use HUD controls; release Ctrl to return to aiming         |
| 1–9 / 0                           | Select the corresponding fitted weapon group directly; see HUD shortcuts   |
| E                                 | Queue AP / HE for next load; double-press to switch now with a full reload  |
| − / +                             | Decrease / increase minimap size (numpad keys also work)                     |
| T                                 | Toggle automatic shell-follow camera; press again to return early            |
| C                                 | Cycle chase, bridge, and tactical cameras                                    |
| R                                 | Recenter camera                                                              |

| H                                 | Hide / show instruments                                                      |
| Hold Tab                          | Helm wheel: the fleet fans out around you; release on a ship to take its helm |

| Shift-D                           | Developer console: live weather and scene diagnostics, in port and at sea    |

**Shift-D** opens the developer console in every build, in port and in battle. Type a setting or command (`wind 14`, `time 6:30`, `clouds 80`, `from 200`, `fog 12`, `storm`, `reset`, `copy diagnostics`) and press Enter, or drag a value chip under the command line to scrub it; double-click a chip to return that value to the scene's. Time of day, cloud cover, wind speed and direction, and visibility change the sky, sea, fog and smoke live. In a local battle or sea trial the Rust sea physics follow the wind from the next batch, so ships ride the sea on screen. Overrides last until the scene changes: entering a battle, returning to port, restarting a mission or resetting a trial restores the scene's weather. While overrides are live, a small DEV tag under the top bar counts them. Online battles keep the server's weather; the console offers diagnostics only there.

Each manually controllable fitted weapon group has its own direct shortcut, ordered main guns first, then secondaries from largest caliber down, followed by torpedoes and depth charges. Matching guns share a group across mount layouts; different firing characteristics, ammunition or aiming envelopes stay separate. Pressing a group's key always selects that group, with no cycling. Slots stay fixed through damage and ammunition loss. Each group retains its own AP/HE choice, and its mount readiness, stocks, flight time and aim markers reflect only its weapons. Guns of 80 mm or less are automatic-only when any larger gun is fitted: they stay on air defense and cannot fire at ships for either players or bots. Ships fitted only with light guns retain manual and bot surface fire. This rule does not change with damage or ammunition loss. Unselected AA guns retain automatic air defense; selecting an eligible AA group gives you manual control. Bismarck uses 1–3 for 380, 150 and 105 mm guns; Fletcher uses 1 for guns, 2 for torpedoes and 3 for depth charges. Number keys are rebindable weapon slots, so their weapon types can differ between ships. The first ten groups have default shortcuts (1–9, then 0); any additional groups remain directly clickable in the HUD. Older saved category bindings migrate to slots 1–4 while other custom controls are preserved.

**Esc → Settings** opens graphics, sound, and **Keybindings** in a separate dialog. Select a primary or alternate binding and press a key; changes apply immediately and are saved in this browser. Esc cancels capture, Delete clears a binding, and Reset restores the defaults. Esc and Enter remain reserved for menus; Shift binoculars, the right-mouse aim lock and Ctrl cursor release remain fixed controls. The HUD and control hints follow your bindings. Graphics changes reload the scene in port. **Close game** closes the tab when permitted by the browser.

**Esc → Settings → HUD** controls battle instrument scaling. Automatic mode is on by default: it fits the HUD to the game window, enlarging it on large displays and reducing it on smaller laptop windows. It uses CSS viewport dimensions, so Retina/OS scaling is already accounted for, and updates on resize, fullscreen changes and moves between displays. Adjust the automatic size from 50–200%, or choose Manual for a fixed size. Changes apply immediately without restarting the battle and are saved in this browser. Ship labels, gun-aim markers, hit readouts and the minimap scale together; port and menu controls retain their normal size. Reset HUD to automatic restores the defaults.

Starting a battle captures the mouse for centered aiming. If the browser declines capture, click the sea to engage it. Esc releases the mouse and pauses; Resume battle captures it again. The third-person sight stays small. Binoculars frame the sea through an opaque eyepiece surround, a recessed lens rim and fine horizontal/vertical sight markings. Range, shell flight time and magnification sit below the crosshair; scrolling still adjusts zoom. The eyepiece stays when H hides instruments and clears for other camera views, inspection and air operations. **Flight time** beside the range estimates shell travel to the sight in seconds for the selected main or secondary battery. It averages the reachable guns’ ballistic solutions, including drag and ship motion, and excludes reload and training time. An em dash means no gun has a usable solution. The scope preserves the aimed position while easing into or out of optics over 0.42 seconds, and mouse sensitivity follows magnification. Scrolling smoothly adjusts camera distance or continuous 1×–32× magnification. Reduced-motion settings make optics transitions immediate. The chase camera orbits upward as you aim down, reaching a near-vertical view over your ship at either zoom limit; its closest distance is 45 m for a Bismarck-sized hull and scales with ship size.

**Scope height:** on surface ships the binoculars look from 30 m above the bridge, whatever the range, gun or ammunition, so the sea tilts toward you a little more than it does from the bridge itself. The eye does not climb for distant targets. Raising the glasses preserves the aimed point and starts at 2×; scrolling changes magnification without changing that point. Sea and terrain clearance still apply, and submarines retain their physical periscope eye.

**Aim lock:** hold the right mouse button to keep the guns on the point they have while the sight looks elsewhere, in the chase view or through binoculars. The gold **Aim locked** ring marks the held point, the numbered gun circles keep reporting against it, and left mouse or Q still fires. Releasing the button swings the sight back onto the locked point. The point is fixed on the sea, so a moving ship still needs fresh lead.

**Free camera:** **O** leaves the ship where the view stands and flies freely: the mouse looks, the helm keys fly (W/S ahead and back, A/D sideways), **E / Q** climb and sink, **Shift** quadruples the speed and the scroll wheel sets it (5–3,000 m/s). The ship keeps her engine, rudder and gun orders, the sight holds its last point, and the HUD fire button still works. The camera may go underwater but not through land. **O**, **C** or **R** returns to the ship with the optics you left. It is rebindable in Settings, is unavailable in port, inspection and the air operations map, and ends when another view takes over.

**Optical rangefinding:** in binocular view with a gun battery selected, aim loosely near a ship and press **G**. Keep the ship within the dashed sight circle for three seconds to acquire its range. The readout names the chosen ship and shows acquisition progress. Press **L** when ready to lock elevation: mouse movement then sweeps left/right for manual lead while the guns and center sight retain the crew’s updated range. The crew continues tracking while the ship is in view; land, intervening hulls, or loss of a current visual report interrupt observations. **Contact lost** holds the last measured range until G reacquires it. L restores free vertical aiming. Leaving optics, changing camera/ship/target, or selecting torpedoes clears the measurement. Both shortcuts are rebindable in Settings. The three-second acquisition and smoothed updates are gameplay approximations of optical rangefinding and rangekeeping; they do not model optical error, smoke attenuation, or a complete predicted fire-control solution.

Press **T** to ride behind a shell from your next salvo along its actual ballistic path. Enabling it with your shells already airborne follows the latest one. The camera holds briefly at the first ship strike or water impact, then restores your previous view and binocular magnification; the option stays on for subsequent salvos. A penetrating shell can continue through the ship while the camera stays on the strike. Your aim stays in place during flight. While following either a shell or an aircraft, move the mouse to orbit around it; if the cursor is released, drag with either mouse button (or touch). Scroll to move the camera closer or farther away. The object stays tracked, and orbit/zoom do not change the ship’s saved aim or optics. Mouse clicks control the follow camera rather than firing or toggling binoculars. Press T again to turn shell follow off and return early. Camera, recenter, Shift binocular and inspection controls can also return you to the ship. Esc pauses both the flight and impact view. The option starts off for each battle, and its shortcut can be changed in Keybindings.

**Numbered gun-aim circles** show the selected battery's current barrel aim at the sight's range, including gravity and ship motion. A round that would fall short is marked at sea level. Amber dashed circles mean the guns are still turning; solid mint circles marked **On aim** mean aligned and loaded. Reloading mounts keep their aim circle with a countdown; blocked, empty and disabled guns have crossed circles. Guns outside their traverse/elevation limits show **Out of arc**, and unreachable distances show **Out of range**. These unavailable guns never show a turning countdown; displayed seconds are explicitly labeled **Reload**. Numbers match the turret row, and converging circles combine their numbers. Arrows indicate guns pointing outside the view, with **Aft** for guns pointing behind the camera. The white center sight remains your commanded aim. These are aiming references, not guaranteed hits: intervening hulls and armor still resolve normally. Circles update every frame in chase and binocular views, and hide during shell follow, inspection, sinking or hidden instruments.

The throttle and rudder stay where you set them. A/D step through full port, half port, amidships, half starboard and full starboard. The compact helm also lets you select any notch directly; its gold marker shows the actual rudder following the order. Going from ahead to astern takes time. Ships accelerate and turn under individual propeller/rudder forces, shape-dependent resistance and loaded mass/inertia. Construction uses catalog fittings at their actual positions; older presets infer missing physical parameters from their authored handling and machinery. Shared gameplay tuning speeds planar force response by ×2, hard-over rudder authority by ×2 and authored rudder shift by ×1.2, retaining heavier acceleration, braking and course changes while straight-line top speeds stay unchanged. Turns produce sideslip and speed loss through water forces, and propeller wash permits low-speed steering when the rudder is behind a working screw. Floodwater changes maneuvering mass, CG and inertia. These are provisional engineering/game approximations; see [maneuvering physics](maneuvering.md). Ships then cover ground and turn 50% faster than their physical motion, and aircraft are paced with them, so the game plays quicker while every speed readout stays real: a 30 kn ship still reads 30 kn, keeps its turning circle and keeps the handling differences between hulls. Gun shells, including AA, fly their same arcs to the same ranges but arrive 25% sooner; muzzle velocities and maximum ranges in port remain the authored figures. Torpedoes run at twice their authored speed over unchanged range and arming distances, and port statistics show the authored speed. These are provisional gameplay adjustments. Four navigational buoys provide nearby reference points; they are markers, not collision obstacles. Enemy ships start at the distance chosen in custom battle setup (5 km by default). Point the sight at a ship, select a battery with 1/2, then hold left mouse or Q to fire. A click fires only aligned, loaded and unobstructed turrets in the selected battery. Holding left mouse or Q admits each turret as it lines up or finishes reloading; releasing stops the request, and an unsuccessful click is never saved for later. Guns outside their aiming limits keep their ammunition and remain loaded. Guns remain blocked when the ship obstructs their actual firing path. The **can fire** count and green aiming circles use the same simulation eligibility check. The ballistic solver and aiming circles share the center of each turret’s barrels, keeping trained circles centered on the sight at every binocular magnification. The ammunition bar shows live shell counts, mount readiness and reload seconds. Hold Ctrl and open the upper-left Enemy roster to select a ship; its hull condition updates live. The transparent chart starts at an 8 km radius and shows friendly ships in mint, enemies in salmon, and a ring around the selected enemy, plus your trail and viewing direction. Use −/+ to resize it, or click its kilometer readout to cycle chart range. Changing graphics settings returns you to port. Losing browser focus pauses singleplayer.
Enemy impacts also show the struck part, impact outcome and actual hull HP lost in compact text just above the hit. Each shell's layers combine, with equipment hits taking naming priority. Visible hits on the same component with the same result share one label, such as **Penetration x2**, with summed hull damage; different components and results stay separate. Zero-damage hits omit the HP line. Armor stops, penetrations and ricochets use distinct icons and repeat counts beside the component name, saving the outcome row: a barred shield, an arrow through a plate, and a deflected arrow. Destroyed components show **Destroyed [component name]** with a red crossed-square icon and repeat count on the same row. Spacing follows each marker's actual height. These labels stay attached to the moving hull, fade over simulation time, and follow pause and H.

Friendly and enemy bots have an overhead name and a thin, compact hull-HP bar with the percentage centered below. Sunk ships show only a faded name. Mint bars identify friendly ships and salmon bars identify enemies. Labels follow the displayed hull poses and hide outside the camera view, in port or with the H instrument toggle. Your own ship has no overhead label. Hull damage appears in gold beside the name and briefly highlights the lost bar segment. Hits arriving together combine into a salvo number; own-ship damage also produces a readout above the helm. Feedback freezes while paused.

Ships use a shared displacement-based hull-durability scale: **Bismarck has 50,750 HP**. Every historical preset and future custom blueprint uses `round(50,750 × (displacement tonnes / 43,978)^0.8)`: a gentle small-ship bonus with no flat base HP. Doubling displacement gives approximately 74% more HP. Hull damage retains its 35× conversion from authored equipment units. Bismarck's 38 cm AP delivers about 1,592 hull damage on a substantial penetration, 367 on a thin through-shot, and up to 2,083 when it damages equipment, before local saturation. Actual hull losses, scores and damage readouts use whole numbers; sub-HP losses carry forward internally. Armor rejection causes no hull damage. Entry, exit, inner plates and delayed bursts share one shell's damage ceiling per ship. HE pays armor protection and does less hull damage; armed torpedoes apply scaled hull damage and open local breaches. Equipment condition and physical flooding retain their own scales. Armed torpedoes reliably detonate at impact angles of at least 20° to the local hull surface. Shallower glancing hits have a seeded dud chance rising linearly to 90% at grazing incidence; failed contacts show “Dud · glancing impact” and cause no damage or flooding. Successful detonations retain full damage before protection and saturation. This is gameplay tuning, not historical torpedo reliability. Torpedo direct damage now uses 62.5% of its authored value and breaches use 7/11 of their authored area. Bismarck, Yamato and King George V have estimated underwater defense zones along their central sides: contacts there halve the remaining damage and breach area. Bow, stern and keel hits outside those zones remain unprotected. Select underwater defense rows in port Armor to see blue coverage volumes and reduction values; these are gameplay estimates, separate from belt armor thickness.

Damage now depends on **local structural condition**. Repeated hits on one section remove progressively less hull HP, while intact regions, equipment behind wreckage and new flooding openings remain vulnerable. Port/starboard, underwater hull, upper hull, upperworks and gunhouses have separate budgets. Equipment repairs do not restore structural capacity. The earlier four-broadside sinking measurement predates saturation; see the local damage calibration and validation.

Generators supply fixed pumps and gun mechanisms. Losing supply slows training and loading, with a 25% manual fallback; portable pumps remain available. Damage to fire-control equipment widens shot dispersion. Bots shift aim away from exhausted empty sections while retaining opportunities to reach intact machinery. Hull exhaustion still causes sinking with a `hull-failure` cause; flooding and capsize remain independent loss paths.

The Damage counter records actual hostile hull HP removed, capped at remaining HP. The last hostile projectile to damage the hull or open a breach earns one frag on irreversible physical loss: hull failure, flooding or capsize. A disarmed or ammunition-exhausted ship remains afloat, movable, targetable and scored. Friendly damage and hits after loss do not add score or steal attribution. Returning to port resets the counters.

**Damage log** sits directly beneath Damage / Frags as three small, transparent text lines (two on compact screens). Each line shows the weapon, other ship and hull HP: **+** for damage dealt, **−** for damage taken. Hold Ctrl to scroll the latest 40 entries or hover a line for battle time, hit count and full weapon details. Hits from the same weapon on the same ship within one second combine into a salvo. Armor rejection and friendly fire do not enter this hostile-damage record. The record pauses with the battle and resets in port; it stays hidden until damage occurs.

The upper-left **Friendly / Enemy** rows show ships afloat out of each team's starting total, plus damaged and lost counts. Hold Ctrl and select a team to inspect a compact inline roster with individual hull percentages and vessel status; select an enemy name to target it. Ship details expand in the same narrow edge column; there is no persistent target dropdown. Duplicate ships have numbered names; your own ship is marked **You**. Gameplay instruments use transparent backgrounds, including weapon controls, chart, carrier and depth controls. Text shadows and instrument outlines preserve readability without panel fills or screen-edge shading.

Bots spend 8–14 seconds acquiring their first firing solution, with small additional delays between gun crews. They observe targets intermittently, lead an estimated course, and alternate their aim between forward, middle and aft areas of the hull. Range and bearing errors shrink during steady tracking; changes in speed or course spoil the solution. Main and secondary mounts fire independently when aligned, unobstructed, loaded and in range, with brief crew delays after reload. Each bot has its own engagement distance and sustained course/speed choices. Taking damage can prompt an evasive turn; heavily damaged bots seek more distance. New battles vary these decisions, while a recorded seed reproduces them. See [bot behavior and tuning](bot-behavior.md).

Bots hold fire through friendly hulls and steer away from nearby ships; shell collisions still apply to all hulls, including allies. Propulsion, steering, magazines, ammunition, flooding and sinking affect every ship. Destroy the opposing fleet or lead on afloat tonnage at the 30-minute limit to win; friendly bots keep fighting if your ship is lost. **Hold Tab** at any time to open the helm wheel: every afloat friendly ship sits around your hull at its true bearing on a log range scale with hull, speed and range. Move the mouse toward one and release Tab, click it, or press its number to take its helm; the ship you leave sails on under its captain. When your ship sinks the wheel opens on its own; Esc keeps spectating instead. The same wheel works while following a captain in fleet command, where the chart's Follow and Take helm remain. Esc opens the return-to-port action after victory or defeat.

Aim AP at turrets or magazines to disable weapons. AP applies 75% of its listed damage on a direct equipment strike, up from 25%, with a separate protected burst after its fuze delay. **Surviving guns keep fighting**, including secondaries after the main battery is destroyed. Main-battery loss adds no special ship status. Disabled turrets stop traversing and elevating at their last simulation angle; they still move with the hull. Temporary magazine flooding can recover when drained. A ship leaves the battle only on irreversible physical loss; loss of propulsion alone does not silence its weapons. Armor still protects machinery; hull failure, flooding and capsize can sink a ship. See the damage and turret behavior update.

Ships collide with friendly and enemy hulls. Contact slows and pushes the ships according to their mass; glancing impacts slide along the hull, and off-center hits can turn it. You can reverse or steer away after contact. Sinking wrecks remain solid until they descend below the other ship's keel. Collision shapes follow a simplified convex envelope of each blueprint's hull stations; ramming damage is not implemented.

**Type VIIC** is an early-war 1941 submarine preset with surface and submerged operation. It has an articulated 8.8 cm deck gun, a 2 cm platform gun, four bow tubes and one stern tube, with 14 torpedoes. Select **2** for torpedoes (selected initially on this preset), aim within ±15° of the bow or stern, then click / Q / Fire for one tube or hold to launch loaded tubes in sequence. The torpedo instrument shows each tube’s readiness, remaining rounds and reload, plus range and arming distance. Manual aiming requires you to lead moving ships, and is by bearing alone: a pale wedge on the water covers every course that would meet the hull nearest the sight, from her stern to her bow, with a bar at the meeting distance. Lay the teal salvo wedge on it and the sight reads **ON TARGET**. G7a torpedoes run straight at about 48 kn, at 2 m depth, for up to 5 km; they arm after 300 m and tubes reload in 45 seconds. Selecting torpedoes on a surface ship lifts the chase view so the sea fills the lower half of the frame. A faint sheet covers each launch arc out to the run limit, and a teal wedge runs down each launcher's course, gold over the unarmed run; a launcher that cannot fire yet shows a dashed outline, and with the sight out of arc a coral outline waits on the nearest limit. The sight reads the running time and distance to the meeting, or the full run with nothing to lead, and says why a launch is held. A chevron on the edge of the view points to a lead out of frame. A water wake and a chevron overhead, mint for friendly torpedoes and coral for hostile ones the team has sighted, show each run; hull contact creates a water plume, damage and flooding. Allies and sinking wrecks can be hit. Bots use the same tubes, lead solver and damage rules. Type VIIC has 1,993 hull HP from the shared displacement curve; flooding remains a separate sinking route. The **Depth** instrument and **U / J** order Surface or 50 m; **Z / X** and the Rise / Dive buttons change the order in 2 m steps up to 150 m. **B / Emergency blow** empties ballast faster and orders the surface. Tanks fill or blow over time, and dive planes help while underway. Depth is measured below the original surfaced waterline, so keel depth includes another 4.74 m. Electric motors supply submerged propulsion at up to 7.6 kn; diesels resume near the surface. Guns are secured below 0.5 m; torpedo launches are allowed down to 12 m and settle onto their 2 m running depth. Deep hulls pass below surface ships and shallow torpedoes. Damage flooding is separate from ballast and enters faster at depth; pressure beyond 150 m opens a persistent hull breach. Combat submarine bots dive before entering torpedo range and remain at attack depth through reloads and turns; they surface after leaving the engagement or exhausting their usable torpedoes. Static and moving target bots remain on the surface. **P** toggles the raised periscope view at your current depth; change its shortcut in Settings → Keybindings. **Shift** also uses the periscope; the zoomed-out chase view follows the hull. Shallow-dive zoom preserves the viewing bearing when the chase sight meets water over your own stern. **C** can also cycle to the unmagnified periscope view. A scope taken deeper stays underwater. Ballast/depth rates are tuned gameplay values; battery charge, finite compressed air, oxygen and sonar are not simulated. See [Type VIIC configuration and limitations](../assets/ships/type-viic/README.md).

**Fletcher** is an original early-war destroyer preset with five single 5-inch guns, two rotating quintuple torpedo mounts and 28 depth charges. Select **2** for ten Mk 15 torpedoes: aim 40–140° from the bow on either broadside, wait for training, then press left mouse / Q for one tube or hold for a sequence. No spare torpedoes are carried. Charges sink to a shallow 10 m gameplay setting before exploding within a 32 m radius, damaging nearby hulls and opening flooding breaches. Make a close pass and keep moving clear; blasts can hit your ship and allies. The HUD shows station readiness, ammunition and reloads. Bot tactics and caliber-based engagement limits are provisional gameplay tuning. Carriers also deploy their playable air groups (see below). Fleet selection is retained during this page session.

**Merchant designs** include the standard EC2-S-C1 Liberty Cargo (11 kn), the structurally different EC2-S-AW1 Liberty Collier (11 kn; machinery/poop aft, detached bridge and ten steel hatch covers), and VC2-S-AP2 Victory Cargo (15 kn; longer/wider hull, raised forecastle, paired kingposts and a geared turbine). Victory is a separate class, not a Liberty cargo variant. Their aft 5-inch main guns use broadside/stern bearings; forward 3-inch and Oerlikons use the secondary battery. **Flower Corvette** now follows the actual HMCS Cobalt general arrangement dated 19 November 1941: short forecastle, raised forward 4-inch, open compass bridge, two tall masts, aft pom-pom and twin Lewis mounts. Gunfire uses caliber-scaled ignition and large fireballs that cool over roughly 0.6–0.8 seconds into drifting propellant smoke. Firing smoke thins after cooling and is mostly gone within about three seconds, before its particles expire at 3.3–4.2 seconds. The smoke uses local raymarched 3D density, erosion and sunward light absorption inspired by the vendored Sky Pro clouds. Elongated gas volumes follow the barrel direction and open into rolling billows, with restrained fine detail and edges that erode into wisps. Smoke responds to daylight, weather and moonlight, including while paused. Adaptive sampling spends more detail on large, dense plumes, with a fixed particle budget; see the smoke quality and GPU review. While binoculars are active, smoke from your ship is hidden, including existing propellant and impact smoke; it keeps aging and returns at its current state when you leave the scope. Smoke from other ships and splash effects remain visible. Shells follow CPU ballistics with incandescent orange bodies, hotter gold noses, soft amber halos and thin, pale trails retaining 1.25 seconds of flight. The glow uses the existing instanced head effects and emissive body shading; shells do not add dynamic lights or a bloom pass, and submerged rounds lose their heat and halo. Head, glow and trail widths scale with caliber as well as camera projection, keeping small rounds visibly finer than main-gun shells. Trails follow observed curves and ricochets, fade after impacts, and adjust their width with range and camera zoom. Trails remain visible when following a shot with T, with near-plane clipping to keep close ribbons thin. Histories survive worker and network snapshots by shell ID and age. Reusable history rings sample at 10 Hz, offscreen segments skip rendering, and vapor trails share a maximum of eight instanced batches (8,192 segments); physical projectiles are unaffected by that cosmetic budget. Close views reveal an original caliber-scaled metal projectile with a curved nose, cap seam, engraved copper driving bands and recessed base; distant rounds use a simpler silhouette. See the shell tracer and model review. Solid armor strikes produce a bright ignition flash and caliber-scaled, rolling orange fireballs that cool into drifting soot over roughly 0.6–0.9 seconds; ricochets retain a sharp directional spark spray and smaller cold smoke plume. Impact smoke thins within about three seconds; water impacts throw up long directional streaks and a low outward crown, scaled by caliber and inclined along the incoming shell. Curved water sheets stretch and tear into falling spray under gravity; fast drops smear like rain and round out at their apex. For guns larger than about 11 cm, the stalled tips shed a soft sunlit cap, the collapsing column leaves a misty curtain and the returning water rolls a low spray surge out across the sea. That spray drifts downwind for 10–15 seconds after the sheets have fallen, over a slick of foam on the displaced ocean that fades over about 20 seconds. Splashes start at the CPU impact’s water height and respond to battle lighting. Visible streaks retain their detail at every zoom level; offscreen water keeps aging. See the water-impact animation review. Magazine detonations have a separate effect. Pause freezes the effects and returning to port clears them. Visual scales are gameplay approximations; see the effects review record.

When your ship is hit, a salmon arc around the sight points toward the incoming shell relative to your camera, including hits from behind. Nearby hits share a cue; separate directions can appear together. Armor stops and ricochets count too. The markers hold briefly, fade over 2.2 seconds total, freeze on pause, and follow the H instrument toggle.

Shell strikes also leave lasting marks on the ship itself: penetrations have dark punctures and torn paint, stopped AP rounds leave steel dents, and ricochets score a scrape along the incoming direction. Larger calibers leave larger marks. Scars conform to the visible mesh and follow hull movement, sinking and turret rotation. They persist while paused, hide in Armor/Internals inspection, and clear when the ship resets or returns to port. Each ship retains its latest 96 marks, batched by struck mesh. AP and HE use their respective strike marks, with broader scorch marks for HE contact bursts. These are surface decals and gameplay approximations, not holes cut through the model.

## PvE fleet command

Choose **Battle** in port and the **Fleet command** tab to assemble task groups, then place ships or whole formations on the circular deployment chart. Each team is limited to 200,000 tonnes, 15 ships and 100 embarked aircraft. Enemy strength scales to the selected fleet; its composition and deployment stay private and remain fixed while you arrange your ships. **New opponent** explicitly changes the mission seed.

Setup and deployment fill the screen. Drag catalog ships into **Group 1**, **Group 2**, and subsequent groups, or drag existing ships between groups; dropping on the **+ Group** lane starts a new group, and each group has a name and a front or rear station. The allowance meters sit above the lanes, and the waters, weather, difficulty and mission seed sit in the rail. **Deploy fleet** opens the same deployment chart as custom battle, with each task group as a frame: drag frames or tags to move groups, ship markers to move single ships, and the compass ring to rotate; the Group / Ship scope, heading dial, keyboard nudges, undo and reset work the same way. Scroll to zoom and drag empty water to pan. Placements outside the shaded friendly sector, near land or within 350 m of another ship disable **Start battle** until fixed.

In M mode, **H** hides the instruments while drag, scroll, arrow-key and edge camera movement remain available. Hidden instruments cannot issue orders or change selection. In fleet command, **left-drag pans**, **Shift-left-drag box-selects**, and **Ctrl/Cmd-drag changes the camera angle**. Middle-drag also pans; scroll zooms, arrow keys pan, and Shift-arrow keys change angle. **Move** previews a line to the pointer; click water to place a waypoint, or Shift-click to append. Move stays active after placing a waypoint; press Esc to finish. Hold Shift to extend the preview from the last waypoint and append there on click; release Shift to preview a replacement destination from the ship or formation center. Shift-drag still box-selects outside Move mode. Owned ship routes stay visible after deselection. Blocked routes use a heavier dashed brass line, with a warning triangle and “Route blocked” beside the ship on the chart; the own-fleet panel and bottom roster also flag the ship without selection. These warnings clear when navigation resumes. Route bends and formation outlines have rounded display corners. Friendly groups, including rear carrier groups, start on station without predefined waypoints; escorts retain their group assignments. Aircraft map markers share a minimal health bar, visible only when selected or close enough to inspect. **Aircraft** opens the air panel with carrier buttons and aircraft thumbnails. Box-selecting any friendly plane selects its flight, even from the Ships tab; boxes containing both ships and planes prefer the active tab. Ctrl/Cmd+Shift-drag adds to the selection. Strike and intercept lines point to the assigned contact rather than the pilot’s approach waypoint.

Fleet command separates selecting a ship, following its camera and taking its helm. Take helm restores the regular sailing, gunnery and minimap controls; Give back helm returns control to the captain. Captains navigate and operate guns/AA; torpedoes start held. Routes, holds, escorts, focus fire, weapons policy, named control groups and local tactical pause are available. Spotting is binary: an enemy within a friendly lookout’s range and unobstructed line of sight is immediately identified and visible. Surface lookout range scales from 9 km for small ships to 11 km for large ships. Gunfire adds 1 km for 20 seconds after the latest shot; weather, horizon and terrain still limit sightings. Aircraft range also depends on target size/altitude. Ship cameras require that ship’s own lookout; fleet command shares friendly sightings. Visible enemy ship and aircraft markers show sampled HP bars and percentages; contact popovers show HP remaining. HP disappears when sight is lost. Visible ship and aircraft poses refresh at 10 Hz and animate smoothly between samples. Losing sight removes the model and leaves a last-known map report; hidden motion stays private.

Local PvE offers **1×, 2× and 4×** simulation speed in fleet command and at the helm. All authoritative systems advance in fixed ticks; cameras remain responsive. Tactical pause stops the simulation and retains queued orders. Restart and new battles return to 1×; online timing is unchanged.

Escorts anticipate turns and changing leader speed, reform around obstacles, and report damaged stragglers. Select their leader to choose **Slow for stragglers** or **Leave behind**; the escort's standing assignment remains intact. Captains temporarily evade observed aircraft approaches and locally spotted torpedo wakes. Enemy commanders share reported targets, assign carrier support and reposition surviving forces after their front collapses.

The restrained **Surface search** overlay records sampled lookout coverage and its age. Point at water for its latest survey time. It represents a reference surface vessel, not guaranteed empty water or coverage of aircraft/submarines. Contacts distinguish current, estimated and last-known positions; visible fire/smoke/list and confirmed sinking appear only when observed. Aircraft uncertainty rings remain absent.

PvE has **no active-airborne-group cap**. Each carrier retains its 48 aircraft and normal launch/recovery queues. The versioned PvE air profile uses **40-minute finite endurance**, recalls at 32 minutes and closes new-task admission at 30 minutes. Mission completion, empty weapons, critical damage and explicit recall can return aircraft sooner; losses remain persistent. See [air operations](air-operations.md) for the timer audit and unchanged legacy defaults.

The desktop MVP is delivered; see [implementation status](pve-implementation-status.md) for acceptance evidence and deferred work. The broader [PvE plan](archive/pve-fleet-command-plan.md) is a roadmap, not the completion checklist. Existing Custom/online rules retain their current behavior.

## Carrier flight operations

Choose **USS Enterprise** or **IJN Shōkaku** in Custom battle and press **M** for **Air operations**. M switches the actual scene camera to a high, north-up view of the battlefield, tilted 20° from vertical, with no grid. The camera rises into the map and descends back to the ship over 1.4 seconds; reduced-motion preferences keep the switch immediate. Map markers and routes track the rendered camera every frame. Command/Ctrl-drag or middle-mouse drag to orbit; Shift+arrow keys also change the angle. Orbit adjusts tilt from overhead to 80° from vertical. **Shift-drag** draws a box around your airborne squadron positions to select them together. **Command/Ctrl-click** a squadron card, map label or manifest row to add or remove it; Command/Ctrl+Shift-drag adds a box to the selection. A normal click selects one squadron, or deselects it when it is the only selection. Orders and Return apply to every selected squadron through the existing role, readiness and flight-slot checks; feedback reports partial acceptance. Selected groups share gold highlights and show their routes. Camera dragging, arrow keys, edge panning, zoom and orbit remain available when **H** hides the instruments. Drag, hold arrow keys, or move the mouse to a screen edge to pan; scroll to zoom. M or **Return to ship** restores the previous ship view; Esc first cancels a pending target action and otherwise returns to the ship. Combat continues; aiming and firing are suspended in the overhead view.

Shōkaku carries **48 aircraft: 16 Zeros, 16 Vals and 16 Kates**. Both carriers use an equal-role gameplay complement globally, including Custom and online; these counts are not historical manifests. See the [Shōkaku configuration notes](../assets/ships/shokaku/README.md) for model limitations.

Enterprise carries **48 aircraft: 16 Wildcats, 16 Dauntlesses and 16 Devastators**, divided into **nine initial legacy groups (6, 6 and 4 aircraft per role)**, with up to four active at once. In M view, the compact bottom row shows each squadron’s number, baked image of its actual aircraft model, aircraft remaining and status. A **wing manifest** at the right edge lists every squadron with one cell per aircraft: the cell fills with condition, colored by status, and its underline shows the remaining bomb, torpedo or gun ammunition; hover a cell for the numbers. Its header totals surviving aircraft, active flights, deck and hangar occupancy and the recovery queue, and each row shows the squadron’s activity, mission and endurance. Click a row to select that squadron, or use **Hide** to collapse the manifest to its header. Click a box or press **1–9 while in M view** to select it; click the sole selected squadron or press its number again to deselect and cancel pending targeting. The normal ship view shows the carrier’s gun selection and firing controls; these numbers select weapons there. Aircraft start hidden in the hangar; a launch order introduces aircraft at the deck’s launch datum. With a clear deck and an available active-flight slot, a full squadron completes its staggered launch in **about ten seconds**. Other launches can queue behind it. Losses last until the next battle. Once survivors are in the hangar, depleted squadrons of the same model and role combine if they fit one six-plane group. The earlier squadron keeps its ID; the command row updates its numbered shortcuts. Damaged aircraft take longer to service and repair up to 60 HP; aircraft above 60 HP retain their health. The Air wing card beside ammunition shows the M shortcut and opens carrier commands.

With a squadron selected, **right-click water to launch or move it to a loiter station**. Bombers retain their payloads while loitering; right-click an enemy ship to strike it. Fighters can right-click a friendly ship to defend it, an enemy squadron to intercept it, or another friendly squadron to escort it. Role and target checks happen in the CPU simulation. Orders persist when you change the selected ship or leave M view. **L** selects loiter, **A** strike, **D** defend, **I** intercept and **E** escort; then click the water, ship or squadron to assign the target. These contextual keys and the matching buttons are available in M view, according to aircraft role. **Esc** cancels a pending action before exiting the map. **R / Return** recalls the selection, **X / Recall all** also cancels queued launches, and **V / Aircraft** opens individual aircraft inspection. **Aircraft → Follow** follows a visible individual plane; **Return to ship**, C or R restores the ship view.

Airborne squadrons have name tags with their remaining aircraft and live activity in both camera modes. M view additionally shows a fixed water-surface cross and circle at each ordered loiter station, separate from the aircraft’s changing position, and a route for the selected squadron.

Returning survivors fly an approach circuit, land, decelerate and move into the hangar to service for 35 seconds. Recovery uses a compressed 60 m final spacing and 1.2-second rollout to support the faster launch cycle. The hangar transfer and overlapping launch runs are deliberate gameplay abstractions, without a deck collision solver or elevator animation. Carrier damage, excessive list or immersion, can suspend launches. Lost aircraft never respawn during a battle.

Bombs use ballistic flight, armor, protected blasts and shared damage/scoring. Finned bombs turn into their falling trajectory; fighter tracers travel in short glowing bursts. Shot-down aircraft descend with a smoke trail and strike the sea with spray and foam. Torpedoes fall into the water, run straight, arm after 180 m and use hull contact and flooding. Surviving high-angle ship guns automatically track aircraft and fire AA bursts. These flight speeds, payloads, damage, AA and reduced air-group counts are gameplay tuning, not a historical reconstruction or a flight simulator. See [air operations](air-operations.md).

Registered high-angle guns up to 130 mm now engage hostile airborne aircraft automatically. Bismarck has eight twin 105 mm, eight twin 37 mm and twelve single 20 mm AA mounts connected to its existing original models. Guns train and elevate, fire visible traveling tracers, consume their shared ammunition, and respect damage, magazine supply, obstruction and friendly-lane checks. The two upper quad 20 mm fittings remain decorative. Light AA tracers continue beyond their aim point for at least three seconds unless they reach the sea, fading over their final 0.65 seconds. Heavy AA ends in a dark, expanding smoke puff at its calculated burst point after the flight time; the smoke drifts with wind and remains visible through binoculars. Active tracers survive combat-log cleanup and grow into additional instance batches during busy firing. Air defense uses rare, stronger hits against aircraft HP. Automatic AA crews and fighter pilots can temporarily panic, waste bursts with poor aim, and recover. Effective ranges, hit rates and burst damage are gameplay calibration; heavy AA uses an approximate timed-burst hit radius, with damage still resolved when firing. Other ships participate according to their registered guns; visual-only fittings do not supply invisible AA damage.

The ongoing 60 FPS work, live measurements and verification limits are recorded in the 60 FPS performance review.

Default battle visibility now fades across 45–55 km depending on the map, keeping hull contrast at 20 km starts; selected weather presets retain their own visibility settings. Clear / Moderate / Storm wave amplitude multipliers are 0.12 / 0.24 / 0.48, with peak wavelengths 12 / 20 / 36 m; sheltered port waves retain their existing amplitude and wavelength. These are visual gameplay settings, not measured historical sea states.

### Naval mechanics calibration

Every fleet gun now has an authored drag profile. AP ricochets continue along a reflected path with reduced energy, and water entry permits a short, rapidly decelerating underwater run that can hit the hull. Armed fuzes continue timing through deflection and immersion. HE bursts at water entry. These are deterministic gameplay approximations, not certified exterior or underwater ballistics.

Fletcher and Type VIIC now use the shared hull-derived stability and damage-control profiles. Ramming and grounding can damage local hull regions and open flooding breaches. Ground clearance samples keel stations and responds to draft and heading. Sustained turns lose speed and develop sideslip; yaw responds gradually, and an unpowered ship coasts down. Weather drives CPU hull motion, wind leeway and heading-dependent wave resistance, with weaker wave motion at submarine depth.

There is no global active-count launch cap on shells, torpedoes, depth charges or aircraft. Ammunition, reloads, carrier operations and each weapon's normal lifetime/range still govern availability. Rendering grows in small instance batches; large battles still cost CPU and GPU time. See implementation and validation notes.

After your ship sinks in a custom battle, the camera automatically follows a surviving teammate. Use the **left/right arrow keys**, the on-screen arrow buttons, or the **Spectating teammate** selector in the battle HUD to switch ships. Click the sea to look around, scroll to zoom, and hold Ctrl to use the controls. If the watched teammate is lost, the camera selects another survivor. The HUD follows the watched ship’s name, health, weapons, helm and navigation position. Your battle score stays yours. Teammates remain under bot control; returning to port clears spectating.

Damageable equipment includes gun mounts, machinery, steering, magazines, generators, directors and supported torpedo/depth-charge launchers. Internals lists equipment and its connected weapons; Flooding has a separate compartment view. Directors serve named batteries and leave local gun control available when damaged. The eight-barrel pom-pom component supports finite ammunition and automatic AA in its retained review prototype. KGV's live pom-pom and UP fittings remain decorative behind the station-evidence gate. Lasting historical fitting uncertainties belong in each ship README; functional inventories come from its blueprint. See the [equipment contract](ship-runtime-contract.md#damageable-exposed-equipment-and-coverage-additive-version-1-extension).
