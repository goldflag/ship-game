## Coordinate and component contract

Blueprint and runtime data use meters, +Y up, -Z bow, +X starboard, with the reference waterline at Y=0. Heading is clockwise from north; the rendered root uses Euler YXZ with yaw `-heading`, pitch `pitch`, roll `roll`.

Blender recipes author in the existing Bismarck frame: +X bow, +Y port, +Z up. The common exporter changes basis for mesh vertices and every local object frame, then uses glTF's Y-up export. The final mapping is `(runtimeX, runtimeY, runtimeZ) = (-blenderY, blenderZ, -blenderX)`. The runtime must not add another model rotation.

Each gun mount exports stable `nodeId` custom properties:

```text
<mount-id>.yaw
  <mount-id>.left.elevation
    <mount-id>.left.recoil
      <mount-id>.left.muzzle
  <mount-id>.right.elevation
    <mount-id>.right.recoil
      <mount-id>.right.muzzle
```

`barrelCount` in the component catalog selects one, two, three or four independently articulated barrels. Omitting it retains the original twin layout. Triples add the `center` chain between `left` and `right`; singles use `center`; quadruples use `left-outer`, `left`, `right`, `right-outer`. `barrelSpacing` is the distance between adjacent bore axes. Simulation, renderer bindings and export checks use the same ordered IDs, so adding a preset does not require ship-name branches in combat code.

Optional `gunhouseShape`, `gunhouseBaseHeight`, `rollerRadius`, `rangefinderWidth` and barrel dimensions let components retain distinct original geometry while sharing joints. A mounting's yaw origin may be a documented roller datum below the gunhouse floor; keep this distinction explicit when authoring its vertical placement.

`gunhouseMesh.version: 1` supports an original closed enclosure of explicitly wound triangular facets. Each facet carries a stable ID, finish, armor material and thickness. Blender renders these facets and the compiler derives the corresponding yaw-local armor plates. Do not also maintain copies of those moving plates in the blueprint. This keeps the visible roof, shoulders and walls aligned with their protection as a component changes.

Runtime yaw rotates around +Y with a negative clockwise angle; elevation rotates around +X; visual recoil translates along +Z. Fixed barbettes remain outside the yaw joint. The `hull.surface` node identifies the actual hull envelope for measurement. `assemblyId` records logical ownership of mesh pieces. Non-rendering simulation objects use `exportRole = simulation` and are excluded from the GLB.

Flexible gun covers may export shape keys with `gunCoverElevationId` naming the
retained elevation joint and `gunCoverAngles` listing increasing positive angles
in degrees, one per target; the base shape is zero degrees. `ShipView` blends
adjacent shapes using the displayed CPU gun angle. These meshes remain outside
rigid batches. Authoring drivers provide the equivalent Blender inspection pose;
export preserves every shape across the coordinate conversion and freezes the
initial driver weights. Cloth deformation never changes firing or hit geometry.

Batching preserves parent and assembly boundaries. Empty joints and sockets survive export. Gameplay identifies components by stable IDs, never GLB node indices or human-readable names. Export checks verify the actual GLB hull bounds, pivot positions, hierarchy, and every muzzle position at three angular configurations per mount. The initial performance guardrails are 500,000 triangles and 30 MiB per model; they are regression guardrails, not a validated fleet-performance promise.

Yamato additionally has `bun assets/ships/yamato/check-dimensions.ts`. It intersects the published hull triangles at the waterline and midship to measure waterline length/beam and hull depth as well as the outer envelope. Its report records individual source references and conflicts. Such checks verify selected dimensions; they cannot certify every hull section, dated fitting or historical proportion.

All per-ship materials that need special handling must have an export path. The existing procedural teak shader is baked; the original starter uses basic supported materials. Add material handling deliberately rather than silently accepting unsupported Blender shaders.

## Blueprint and simulation contract

`ShipBlueprint` is editable, versioned JSON. `compileShip` accepts unknown input, validates it and creates a separate `ShipDefinition` containing resolved part definitions. Compilation checks schema versions, IDs, known parts, finite numbers, bounded lists, station ordering, envelope bounds, module containment, capacity, and compartment/magazine links. These are authoring checks; additional economic, structural, and placement rules will be required before accepting competitive player designs.

`ShipState`, mount state, modules, compartments, shells, and events contain serializable numeric/string data. The simulation has no browser, React, Three.js, Blender, or GPU dependency. `CombatSimulation.step` is the fixed-tick host entry point. The browser currently runs it locally; the server and network command protocol are future work.

### Gun firing and ammunition

Moving main and secondary mounts share the same implementation. `src/simulation/gunnery.ts` operates every mount once per fixed tick: automatic anti-aircraft control claims a registered high-angle mount first unless the player has selected its group directly, otherwise the mount follows the sight, its bot's track, or keeps reloading; a salvo from either path spends one complete load through `expendSalvo` in `weapons.ts`, which owns the shared AP/HE stock. Player control groups are derived by `src/ships/weaponGroups.ts` from compiled weapon firing profiles, independent of mount health or ammunition. Mount layout alone does not split matching guns. The CPU intent carries a stable `weaponGroupId` alongside the battery category; firing, shell selection and telemetry use that group. Omitting the group retains the aggregate battery API for simulation hosts. Invalid group IDs cannot fire the aggregate battery. Group ordering is fixed by fitted definitions, and the browser maps numbered slots to that order. Selected guns stay under manual control while unselected guns retain automatic AA. Their armor, traverse/elevation rates and limits, reload, ammunition, muzzle velocity, penetration budget, damage, and recoil come from the part catalog. Magazine connections can disable a mount. The main HUD and Gunnery panel share the AP/HE load command; the rebindable E action changes only the selected gun battery. The compact HUD cycle control exposes both stocks on hover or focus; Gunnery retains direct AP/HE selection. Mount and weapon counters report rounds for the current load, including AP fallback on guns without HE. A single press queues the next load, preserving the current salvo and reload progress. Two presses within 300 ms force the queued type immediately and pay the complete catalog reload interval. Orders persist per weapon group; returning to port restores the default AP selection. Aiming uses the same gravity as shell flight, includes ship velocity, checks the actual muzzle and mount limits, and tests obstruction against the modeled superstructure and other gunhouses. Player and bot firing share per-mount eligibility: the requested ballistic solution must be reachable within traverse/elevation limits, the actual barrel path clear, and train/elevation aligned within the shared small angular tolerance. Loaded and functional mounts become ready only when these checks pass. Turning, out-of-arc, out-of-range, blocked, empty and disabled states explain why a gun cannot fire; reload time continues independently. A click is consumed on the next simulation tick, while held fire admits each mount as it becomes eligible. The ballistic solver, bot lead calculation and gun-aim overlay use the same average muzzle center; individual shells still spawn at their own muzzle sockets. Obstruction proxies are coarse and need refinement against the visual ship for narrow clearances.

### Armor and structural coverage

Armor supports optional convex planar `plate` surfaces within the version 1 blueprint, with thickness, material, exterior-breach flag and optional mount ID. Plate vertices use ship coordinates for fixed protection and yaw-local coordinates for moving turret protection. The compiler validates planarity, convexity, bounds and mount links. Legacy box armor remains supported for other presets.

Collision coverage is separate from the armor schedule. Bismarck, Yamato, Baltimore and Enterprise enable `structuralPlating` with explicitly provisional ordinary-steel thicknesses: hull/structure 20/8 mm for Bismarck, 25/8 mm for Yamato, 16/6 mm for Baltimore and 15.875/6 mm for Enterprise. The CPU derives each complete hull shell from `hull.sections` and deckhouse shells from `structures[].footprint`; optional original `surface` vertices/triangles describe tapered towers, open funnel jackets and Enterprise's cambered deck/elevator slabs. The visual recipes consume those same surfaces. These cached, spatially bounded surfaces participate in swept hits, sight aiming and Armor inspection. AP can pass through thin structure, losing speed and possibly arming its fuze; equipment and bursts determine local damage. Only hull-shell and explicit exterior penetrations create sea breaches. Nearby exterior armor replaces nominal shell plating instead of charging both at the same location. Combat and the opaque inspection skin share a 1.5 m replacement allowance for reconstructed exterior plate offsets; inspection removes covered skin patches so belt colors and hover picks remain visible. The waterline splash check uses the hull interior, not armor inspection bounds. Broad end sections receive complete caps; zero-breadth air above a recurved bulb receives no fictitious centerline steel. Definitions without this opt-in use the supported legacy armor and optional stability-shell behavior.

Bismarck uses spatially separated belt/backing/support, upper belt, decks, turtleback, bulkheads, barbettes and gunhouse plates. Yamato, Baltimore and Enterprise have their own qualified fixed protection; Bismarck, Yamato and Baltimore's closed gunhouses use original per-facet protection. CPU collisions, sight picking, probes and inspection all use the same plate surfaces; mounted plates follow CPU yaw. A plate consumes resistance once per crossing, including coplanar seams and tick boundaries. Teak contributes no invented steel-equivalent resistance. Catalog profiles author AP arming/delay and AP/HE fill and fragment budgets; material and impact-speed rules remain game calibration rather than certified penetration curves. Local burst rays pay intervening protection. Abstract damage-control teams, finite fuel and supplies live in an optional versioned ship profile; individual crew and mesh fracture are absent. The source/discrepancy registers distinguish corroborated thickness families from estimated boundaries and incomplete schedules.

### Flooding, stability and hull breaches

Compartments retain separate bounded breach clusters and water volume. Explicit exterior regions map openings to flood spaces, splitting waterline openings across room heights. Closed boundaries prevent transfer until opened or penetrated; hydraulic-head transfer conserves water. The original `assets/ships/author-flood-spaces.ts` recipe preserves researched/legacy room IDs and adds estimated wing and end spaces inside the hull. These are inspectable game envelopes, not certified historical watertight subdivisions. The stability authoring recipe adds conservative compound cells for residual upper, deep and end spaces. Cells exclude retained room boxes; the exact historical boundaries, permeability and access routes remain unresolved in the discrepancy registers. Equipment immersion disables availability separately from HP; draining cannot repair destroyed equipment. Explicit propulsion groups link boiler supply, drives and shafts, with provisional aggregation and routing documented per preset. Flooded magazines disable ammunition supply.

The optional versioned stability profile declares dry CG, a uniform buoyancy calibration and default shell thickness. Hull-section clipping derives displacement and buoyancy centers at finite angles. Hydrostatic forces and local roll/pitch restoring-arm slopes update at 2 Hz; CPU pose integration remains at 60 Hz. Between samples, the slopes update torque with attitude so a held restoring force does not numerically amplify short-hull oscillations. Floodwater uses a horizontal free surface over sampled columns in the actual compartment cells. Between hydrostatic updates, pressure and immersion queries invert the full volume/height curve at the last sampled orientation; the centroid refreshes at 2 Hz. Portal transfers stop at equal pressure and conserve water. Inspection queries never mutate simulation state. Moving water supplies the free-surface moment without an extra penalty. The calibration reconciles authored hull volume with stated mass and targets an estimated initial GM; the author-stability helper prints the mismatch and load during calibration. Neither these estimates nor capsize timing establish historical stability.

Loss does not freeze hydrostatic roll or trim. A sinking actor evaluates buoyancy at its current immersion, while the CPU's bounded descent gathers speed from 0.08 to 0.65 m/s, accelerating by 0.015 m/s². An existing downward rate is retained within that speed limit. Pumps stop after loss; breaches and compartment connections still carry water. Later inversion can display `capsized` without replacing the recorded sinking cause or awarding another loss. Wreck motion retires below a hull-size-based clearance depth (1,000 m for submarines), without a seabed or trapped-air model. Definitions without a stability profile keep their capped water-moment approximation, smoothed during sinking. `ShipState.verticalSpeed` starts at zero and resets with the rest of the pose. No renderer-only lean or random death orientation is added.

A cached hull-shell query covers surfaces between explicit exterior plates, including deck, bottom and ends. Bismarck uses its complete structural plating (20 mm hull, 8 mm deckhouses); presets without structural plating use the stability profile's provisional 12 mm hull fallback. A short trajectory probe suppresses the nominal shell where explicit exterior protection already intercepts the path. Unmapped openings reach the nearest actual flood cell. Old Bismarck end-closure proxies are superseded by its complete structural shell. This closes gameplay coverage while leaving detailed plating, downflooding apertures, underwater shock, trapped air and structural fracture unresolved. Hull-shell fallback contacts are labeled in the impact ledger; the port armor overlay shows explicit protection and any authored structural surfaces.

### Local damage, fires and combat loss

The optional version-1 `localDamage` profile authors stable region IDs, bounds, kind, durability fractions and explicit mount ownership. Each region starts with its fraction of maximum hull HP. The first half takes full damage; the remainder tapers exponentially, with no equipment-repair refill. Fixed overlaps select the smallest containing volume; moving gunhouses retain explicit region ownership. The original `assets/ships/author-local-damage.ts` recipe authors these regions and support systems for its declared targets. After adding a preset or changing flood spaces, ensure the recipe covers the affected ship and regenerate its data. Pass explicit IDs (`bun assets/ships/author-local-damage.ts fletcher`) or `all`, derived from the runtime roster. No arguments perform no writes. The helper replaces only its own `support-director` and `support-generator-1/2` IDs, and suppresses those proxies when authored equipment of that kind exists. Rebuild every ship whose authoring inputs change. `Shell.hullRegionDamage` tracks nominal regional ceilings separately from actual `Shell.hullDamage`, so a saturated entry does not spend an intact interior's damage opportunity. Underwater shock distributes one budget over nearby hull regions. See `assets/reviews/local-damage/README.md` for calibration and limits.

Compartment and mount `fire` profiles author finite combustible loads, ignition heat, delivered-heat response and optional visual smoke outlets. Empty voids carry no fuel. Fires consume local fuel and damage equipment; structural saturation does not refill or erase combustible loads. Protected burst queries can reach room fuel without equipment. Closed intact boundaries contain spread; crews can isolate fires through existing portals. Generators occupy existing hull spaces and aggregate electrical capacity for fixed pumps and gun mechanisms. Directors can use the fixed exposed-equipment contract below. Their coverage changes seeded dispersion for all controllers; automatic AA consumes the same director and power inputs. Coverage and equipment protection/HP are gameplay approximations recorded per ship.

Priority/focus orders re-score existing crew jobs without restarting unchanged assignments. `fireReadout` reports the actual assigned crew and setup timer, distinguishes burning from residual heat/cooling, and names local threatened equipment. `LocalizedFireEffects` consumes this existing CPU fire state without another fuel store: mount flames attach to the interpolated ship pose, rooms emit only through authored above-water outlets, and extinction ends new emissions immediately. Existing smoke ages out. Two fixed sprite batches select up to 32 camera-relevant sources across the fleet (96 flame tongues, 512 smoke particles); selection does not change simulation. Pause freezes effect time and returning to port clears effects and crew orders.

Gameplay hull durability is independent of equipment health and buoyancy. The shared displacement curve in `maxHullIntegrity` supplies `round(50,750 × (displacement tonnes / 43,978)^0.8)` HP (Bismarck: 50,750 HP), giving smaller hulls a gentle HP-per-tonne bonus without flat base HP (Type VIIC: 1,993 HP); `durability.ts` calibrates shell consequences without preset-ID branches. Authored weapon damage remains in equipment units; `HULL_HP_SCALE` converts hull consequences to the same 35× scale, including torpedoes and depth charges. Hull losses and scoring are integers, with sub-HP consumption retained on the damage state until it totals a whole point; reset clears that remainder. Precise structural pools preserve saturation, and `Shell.hullDamageConsumed` retains authored-scale consumption so rounding cannot create another penetration budget. Bot evasion thresholds and survivability score references use the same scale. Before scaling and local saturation, AP offers a hull-damage ceiling of 65% of nominal damage after penetrating resistance sufficient to arm its fuze, 15% on a thin through-shot, and up to 85% scaled by actual equipment damage. A burst inside a penetrated hull can upgrade a through-shot to 65%; protected burst damage can upgrade it further according to local damage. HE contact penetration applies 35% of its damage; exposed equipment burst damage can upgrade this to at most 50%. Rejected armor contacts and misses do no hull damage. `Shell.hullDamage` tracks one cumulative ceiling per victim across entry, exit, internal layers, ticks and burst; each `ImpactRecord.hullDamage` records only newly removed HP, separately from local equipment `damage`. Armed torpedoes distribute their weapon's nominal damage ceiling over nearby structural regions once; local saturation limits the HP actually removed.

The helm and overhead bars show hull HP, and Gunnery reports hull and equipment condition separately. Equipment repair cannot regenerate hull HP. Reaching zero HP causes `hull-failure` sinking through the shared CPU sinking pose. Exhausting derived buoyancy or sustained finite-angle inversion can still cause flooding loss or capsize with HP remaining. This is an explicit gameplay abstraction, not simulated structural fracture or a historical survivability claim. Immobile ships can keep fighting. Ships with permanently lost weapons/ammunition leave the battle's fighting strength while afloat; temporary immersion can recover. Every loss retains a cause. Hostile hull damage earns score capped at remaining HP; the last hostile damaging or breaching projectile earns a single frag on permanent combat loss or later sinking. Friendly damage and hits after loss do not add score or steal attribution. Calibration and seeded measurements are recorded in `assets/reviews/damage-realism/gameplay-balance.md`.

Fighting strength includes every gun mount and torpedo tube. Losing the main battery does not silence surviving secondaries or add a special ship status. A gun needs enough AP or HE rounds for a complete mount salvo and a surviving magazine to be recoverable. Temporary immersion remains recoverable; only permanent loss of all weapons or their ammunition removes an afloat ship from battle. Such actors cease fire and receive neutral helm orders, retain collisions and damage control, and can sink later. A ship can lose its weapons or buoyancy with hull HP remaining, or exhaust hull HP while some equipment survives.

Gun and magazine failures publish the affected mount's disabled status during the damage tick. Disabled mounts hold their CPU train and elevation; the renderer uses these angles directly instead of completing an interpolated turn after the failure. Hull motion still carries the disabled gun with it, and recoil interpolation remains independent. Temporary magazine flooding permits normal aiming to resume once the supply recovers.

### Impact events

The impact ledger (`impact`) and renderer attachment evidence (`surfaceImpact`) are separate serializable fields. Surface positions, normals and incoming directions use ship-local coordinates or the struck gunhouse's local frame. Shell effects preserve ammunition type and incoming velocity even when a plate stops the projectile. A projectile spends one kinetic equipment-damage budget across its path; AP uses 75% of nominal damage, with its protected delayed burst handled separately. Exhausting that budget does not remove its remaining speed or cancel an armed fuze. Plate resistance, fuze timing, burst occlusion and physical breach areas are unchanged by this balance coefficient.

### CPU poses, fleet behavior and ship collisions

Battle weather supplies a deterministic renderer-free long-wave envelope (`sea.ts`). It drives heave, roll and pitch through the stability solver, local breach water levels, shell water crossings, wind leeway and heading-dependent resistance. Mean sea level remains Y=0. `ShipState.waveHeave` tracks transient displacement separately from mean flotation: depth, draft and surfaced/submerged classifications use `meanHullY`/`hullDepth`, while collision geometry retains actual world-space Y. GPU detail renders the sea and animates buoys, while combat ship poses remain authoritative CPU values. For smooth motion at arbitrary display rates, the browser interpolates hull and joint transforms between the last two fixed ticks, presenting ships one tick (16.7 ms) behind simulation time. The camera, wake and inspection geometry follow those same sampled ship poses; firing and hit resolution continue to use the current authoritative tick. Pause holds the interpolation fraction, and resets/port teleports clear pose history. GPU wave samples never alter these poses. The CPU envelope shares weather parameters with rendering, but does not reproduce every GPU crest. Omitted weather in low-level simulation fixtures means still water; the battle setup explicitly supplies its weather choice. Custom battles use renderer-free bot helm and fire commands, team-aware targeting, per-mount ballistic leading and shared damage/flooding for every selected ship. Formation lines start at the configured separation (1–20 km, 5 km by default), with both teams facing the opposing line. The simulation retains that distance across resets. Bot crew memory includes delayed observations, estimated velocity, aim error, acquisition/cadence timers and sustained maneuver orders. All decisions use a per-actor seeded generator and simulation time; the browser supplies a fresh battle seed, while explicit seeds and resets reproduce behavior. See [bot behavior](bot-behavior.md). Bot engagement ranges and avoidance are provisional gameplay heuristics. Carrier aircraft operations use the optional versioned airWing extension; networking and a shipbuilding UI are not implemented here.

Ship contacts resolve at 60 Hz after all hulls move and before weapon solutions and hits. `collisions.ts` derives a convex horizontal footprint from each definition's `hull.halfBreadths` in the documented stern-station frame. Hull mass plus floodwater weights separation and inelastic impulses; a rectangular waterplane approximation supplies yaw inertia. Impacts change forward speed, starboard `swaySpeed` and yaw rate, with water resistance settling sideways motion. Ballistic inheritance and target leading include this drift. Contacts apply equally to all teams and controllers. A conservative deck-to-keel vertical envelope, expanded for list/trim, allows sufficiently submerged wrecks to clear other ships. This is a discrete, planar gameplay model with convex envelopes, not mesh contact or detailed hydrostatics. It uses no GPU wave samples. Closing contact dissipates energy into localized hull damage and positional breaches; resting overlap does not repeatedly inflict impact damage. Grounding uses keel samples against an offshore continuation of the authored coast slope, includes draft/heading, and damages the hull above a minimum inward impact speed. Glancing ground contacts remove inward velocity while retaining motion along shore. Normal sailing distances per tick are small compared with the registered hull widths; arbitrary teleports or extreme externally injected speeds are not swept collision queries.

### Renderer bindings, diagnostics and inspection

Surface ships follow the current CPU wave height every 60 Hz tick with a 1.5-second heave response. The expensive flotation solve stays at 2 Hz; its mean-height corrections also ease in, retaining the 1 m/s limit. This avoids abrupt vertical-speed impulses in inherited shell velocity and gun-aim circles while preserving wave motion and flooding settlement.

Target inspection draws armor, modules, compartments and floodwater as an X-ray overlay, including spaces below the visible sea. Development builds expose the read-only `window.shipTrialDiagnostics()` check for the loaded muzzle positions and recent combat events. Muzzle errors compare the exported joints with the CPU poses sampled for the displayed frame. Regression tests load the exported joint hierarchy and check it against simulation poses, including rear-facing turrets, recoil and interpolated turns through north. Frame-loop tests verify smooth sailing across fixed and irregular display intervals without changing combat state.

Surface-hit events additionally retain shell type (AP by default), caliber, outcome and local impact position/normal/direction. Fixed impacts use ship-local coordinates; mounted impacts use the stable mount ID and yaw-local frame sampled at collision time. `ShipImpactMarks` projects these onto nearby, outward-facing visual triangles and parents batched decals to the actual struck mesh. Internal module damage and non-exterior physical armor do not create exterior decals. A proxy without a matching visible face within 3 m leaves no floating mark. The latest 96 marks per ship survive movement and pause, hide for inspection and clear on damage-state replacement. Rendering never changes damage, flooding or the blueprint. `scripts/diagnostics/ship-impacts.html` provides a repeatable development review on the real ocean scene; `renderedShips` diagnostics expose mark and batch counts.

Diagnostics also identify the loaded ship/hash, renderer backend and camera matrices for reproducible browser review. In the development port, `window.shipTrialArticulation({trainFraction: 1, elevationFraction: 1, recoilFraction: 1})` previews the catalog limits on the actual loaded model. Train spans -1 to 1; elevation and recoil span 0 to 1. Passing `null` restores the original mount state. Launching restores it automatically. This review hook is unavailable in production and cannot change joints during combat.

Use `mounts` to pose neighbors independently, for example `window.shipTrialArticulation({trainFraction: 0, elevationFraction: 0, recoilFraction: 0, mounts: {'main-3': {trainFraction: .6}, 'bofors-turret-3': {trainFraction: -.7, elevationFraction: .8, recoilFraction: 1}}})` on Iowa. Overrides use stable mount IDs and the same bounded fractions. Unknown IDs and nonfinite values are rejected before any pose changes.

A mount may declare `parentMountId` to ride another mount's yaw assembly. Parents must precede children in the blueprint; missing parents and cycles are invalid. Position and bearing remain absolute neutral ship-space datums. The exported child base preserves that neutral frame beneath the parent yaw, while CPU mount frames compose every ancestor's train for aiming, hits, fire effects and lodged projectiles. Child elevation and recoil remain independent. Optional mount `traverseDeg` narrows the reusable catalog part's half-sector about the installed bearing; it cannot exceed the part's mechanical range. Optional `traverseLimitsDeg: [min, max]` represents asymmetric installed travel relative to that unchanged bearing. The interval must contain neutral and fit inside the installed/catalog half-sector. CPU aiming, port preview and Statistics use the same limits; interpolation travels through the interval without wrapping across its stops. Rust/WASM gun operation applies the same asymmetric interval.

Optional `mountClearance` version 1 declares installation motion interlocks. Its selected hull-mounted guns carry a barrel radius and optional yaw-local body box; named authored structures supply platform footprints and explicit bulwark height extensions; neighbor pairs identify independently moving assemblies. The basis states the provisional geometry assumptions. TypeScript port previews and the Rust combat kernel both enclose the full recoil stroke, test platform prisms and moving neighboring gun envelopes, and subdivide angular movement with an arc-length margin. A blocked elevation can still traverse away. Statistics exposes the interlock, and development articulation previews follow the same constraints. These conservative game envelopes do not establish historical mechanical stops. Visual review must verify that the envelopes cover the actual published fittings and inspect transitions and independently requested neighbor poses; this feature is not an automatic clearance certificate.

Rust `naval-sim` resolves carried frames from authoritative mount trains for both local WASM and online battles. Weapon operation refreshes each child's carrier after its parent traverses; clearance caches include moving carrier poses. The browser reconstructs derived carrier frames in presentation state without modifying retained snapshot delta baselines.

An optional blueprint `viewpoints.bridge` places a ship's bridge camera in runtime coordinates. Its position follows the authoritative ship pose. Use it for offset islands and bridges instead of adding ship-name conditions to the camera or simulation.

`ShipFunnelSmoke` derives visual exhaust outlets from existing authored `structures` with IDs `funnel`, `funnel-jacket`, or `<assembly>-funnel`. Bases, casings and caps do not add emitters. Open surface boundaries locate raked rims; closed jackets use their upper facing lid, and simple extrusions use the top footprint. Positions use the same runtime coordinate frame and displayed CPU hull pose. New player-built ships can use this convention without preset-name conditions. Particle size, density, aggregate propulsion response and wind drift are illustrative; individual boiler-to-uptake routing and thermal exhaust are not simulated. No model geometry or compiled blueprint is modified by the effect.

Port inspection uses the same renderer through `ShipInspection` and the same data through `inspectionEntries`. Armor includes hull protection and moving gunhouses; Internals includes damageable gun mounts, machinery, magazines, steering, generators, fire control and launcher equipment. Director/launcher entries list their connected weapons. Flooding separately lists and renders compartments, keeping their outlines out of the equipment view. The UI reads thickness, health and capacity from the compiled definition, with stable prefixed IDs for selection. Add new inspection properties to this shared adapter rather than maintaining a second port-only layout. Verify each new preset in both port views, isolate a volume, return to Statistics, launch and return to port. The Statistics sheet reads `shipStatistics` and `shipScores` from `src/ships/statistics.ts`; add new figures there so the sheet and its tests share one source. Combat stays frozen in port; entering port resets every fleet actor, ammunition, shells and flooding while preserving renderer bindings. Fleet launches load each distinct preset once and clone independent views for all actors; every hull uses its own definition, armor, modules, mounts and joints. Without a fleet, the simulation constructor retains an idle two-actor fixture for port inspection and isolated asset/combat regression tests; the playable launch always supplies a custom fleet.

Port armor uses `ArmorOverlay` to render opaque plates into a separate depth buffer, then composites them over the faded actual ship and sea before FXAA. Plates occlude deeper armor and show an outline only while hovered. A camera-relative neutral diffuse shader shades geometric faces with ambient fill, preserving the thickness palette independently of harbor lighting and exposure. `InspectionHover` raycasts the visible inspection meshes, including physical thickness and current mount poses, and supplies the tooltip without changing selection or simulation. Internals picks equipment independently of enclosing spaces; Flooding picks compartments. Both highlight the hovered volume. Isolating a list row makes that inner layer available for inspection and hover. Internals and combat inspection keep their translucent presentation.

## What is editable now, and what comes next

Hull station tables, component placements, polygonal superstructure tiers, equipment, physical armor plates and internal layout can be changed in JSON and rebuilt. The Bismarck recipe still has ship-specific superstructure, fittings and underwater details; it is not a generic in-game hull editor. Large hull changes require adjusting those structures and validating module/compartment placement.

The schema gives future editor code a durable authoring boundary. Full construction still requires a hull editor, derived mass/displacement and compartment generation, attachment/clearance rules, undo/redo, persistence migrations and competitive validation. Render-only Blender refinements will not automatically become meaningful construction parameters. Preserve the versioned blueprint rather than trying to reconstruct design intent from the GLB.


## Damageable exposed equipment and coverage (additive version 1 extension)

`Module` retains room containment by default. Only `fire-control` and `launcher` support explicit `placement: "fixed"` with a ship-local `center`/`size` and no `compartmentId`. An optional room association still requires containment. Exposed equipment adds no compartment, flood capacity or buoyancy. `protectionMm` authors its local penetration resistance; omission preserves the legacy 50 mm resistance. These boxes are gameplay hit volumes, not armor certification. Director boxes cover the fixed central station; visual optical/aerial sweeps do not create a moving equipment dependency.

`immersionToleranceM` retains the existing sealed-equipment convention when omitted. When present, the CPU transforms the equipment's lower-center datum plus tolerance through ship heel/trim/height. Room-associated equipment compares that datum to its compartment water level; roomless equipment compares it to the authoritative CPU sea envelope at the current tick. Direct hits, protected bursts, finite-spares repair and reset all support roomless equipment. Room fire/heating/spread remain conditional on a valid room; no new exposed fuel store is implied. Destroyed equipment cannot be rebuilt by repair crews.

Fire-control modules optionally declare `servesMountIds`. If any director declares coverage, every director must provide a list; unknown/duplicate IDs fail compilation. A mount uses the best surviving serving director and existing power factor. Explicitly unserved mounts use local control, adding at most 1.5 mrad dispersion. Director loss never prevents a healthy gun from firing. Legacy definitions without coverage retain the global lookup, including the no-director default. A weakly cached topology resolves coverage once; every query samples current HP/immersion. Aggregate telemetry does not substitute for per-mount firing decisions.

The octuple `pom-pom` component has `barrelCount: 8` and required `barrelVerticalSpacing`, arranged as four columns and two rows on a common elevation axis. Stable IDs are `lower-left-outer`, `lower-left`, `lower-right`, `lower-right-outer`, then corresponding `upper-*` IDs. Vertical bore offsets rotate with elevation; lateral offsets, independent recoil and muzzle positions agree in CPU and Blender. Existing one-to-four barrel IDs/layouts remain unchanged. The trunnion's signed forward coordinate may be aft of the yaw datum. Eight-barrel authoring currently requires the original pom-pom recipe.

Torpedo tubes and depth-charge stations can declare `launcherModuleId`, referring to a `launcher` module. A rotating torpedo bank shares one owner across every tube, with the owner's `torpedoLauncherId` identifying the existing CPU yaw path. Its box is authored in the ship's zero-bearing frame and transformed for direct hits, protected bursts, immersion and inspection. Fixed tubes/racks need no pose binding. A `localDamage` region of kind `launcher` declares `moduleId`; projectile damage ceilings and local saturation still apply once. Equipment contact itself creates no underwater breach or automatic secondary explosion. Owner condition gates aiming/readiness and both usable/recoverable fighting-strength checks. Surviving immersed equipment can recover; destroyed owners cause permanent weapon loss while preserving remaining ammunition.

`bun assets/ships/author-equipment.ts <ship-id> [...] | all` authors the supported original-recipe station bindings and writes `reports/equipment-inventory.json` per ship. It preserves existing compartments, fire loads and local calibration while adding equipment regions. The inventory distinguishes registered equipment from fittings still needing evidence. KGV's `equipment-evidence.json` keeps unverified stations out of the compiled mounts. Its four pom-poms and UP projectors stay decorative; the functional octuple prototype and outstanding model acceptance are recorded in `reports/equipment-review.md`. Fixed directors on other ships use existing original-recipe housing positions, with approximate coverage. Convoy/submarine control proxies and Flower's depth-charge damage owners remain pending a station audit.

## Fixed torpedo tubes (optional version 1 extension)

`assets/parts/guns.json` also contains the optional `torpedoes` catalog. A blueprint may include `torpedoTubes` with a stable ID, part ID, muzzle position, fixed bearing, allowed gyro arc, total per-tube ammunition and magazine link. Compilation resolves a separate torpedo weapon specification. Existing gun mounts keep their main/secondary battery contract; `Battery` adds `torpedo` for player selection. Historical and future player-built ships use the same optional definitions, with no ship-name condition in the combat simulation.

Each fixed tube exports `<tube-id>.muzzle` in the documented coordinate frame. `ship:check` validates its measured position and forward direction. The Type VIIC also preserves independent hydroplane, screw and rudder pivot empties. The diving controller animates these appendages without changing their stable joint IDs.

Torpedoes have separate serializable projectile and tube state. The CPU handles per-tube reload/ammunition, launch spacing, range/arc/arming eligibility, constant horizontal speed, fixed course, depth settling, swept hull collision, contact damage and local breaches. They are not shells with zero gravity: splash termination and AP armor penetration do not apply. The first hull on either team stops a torpedo; impact before arming is a harmless dud. Armed contacts use the swept travel direction and struck triangle normal in ship-local coordinates: incidence at least 20° from the surface always detonates; below 20°, dud probability rises linearly to 90% at grazing incidence. A stateless battle-seed/projectile-ID roll keeps this gameplay tuning deterministic across native and WASM execution. Glancing duds remove the projectile and emit an explicit reason without damage, breaches or scoring attribution; successful detonations have no angle damage multiplier. Runs terminate at their authored range. Contact opens a positional breach through the shared water solver and damages at most one nearby module; the armed warhead also removes hull HP for scoring, without automatic magazine detonation. Flooded supply rooms disable their tubes until drained. Loaded tubes count toward remaining fighting strength. Damage and delayed flooding reuse battle score and sinking rules. Reset removes torpedoes and restores ammunition. Bots calculate constant-speed interception and check predicted friendly motion along the firing lane before launching; helm logic brings fixed tubes to bear while ammunition remains.

The torpedo hull query uses authored section geometry independently of the armor schedule. It resamples unequal point-count rings to 33 vertical samples, and falls back to half-breadth/keel/deck tables when sections are absent. This is an approximate centerline contact query; torpedo diameter, blast propagation, torpedo-defense bulkheads, magnetic pistols and detailed double hulls are not resolved. Range, gyro alignment, arming, reload and damage simplifications are recorded in the ship discrepancy register.

The renderer consumes torpedo launch/hit/dud events and positions to draw underwater bodies, surface wakes and contact water plumes. Audio reuses the original splash and explosion assets with lower playback rates. None of these effects feed the simulation. Torpedo hits also appear in the incoming-hit indicator, hull damage feedback and Damage/Frags counters. The fitted weapon group's direct HUD shortcut selects tubes; existing presets show only the gun batteries. Camera defaults and minimum orbit distance scale with hull length. Port switches preserve orbit direction and relative zoom; target inspection uses the focused hull's size.


## Trainable torpedo mounts and depth charges (optional version 1 extensions)

Fletcher exercises the same blueprint/compiler contract with two additional optional weapon assemblies. `torpedoLaunchers` declares stable IDs, ship-local pivot positions, training rate and permitted launch-bearing sectors. Each of its five `torpedoTubes` references `launcherId`; tube positions are ship-local muzzle positions at zero train. The CPU turns each shared assembly once per tick and derives the transformed tube origin. An excluded launch sector never consumes a round. Fixed submarine tubes retain their submerged muzzle and gyro-arc contract. Deck-launched torpedoes fall to the water, then use the common fixed-depth, straight-course simulation. One-round tubes carry no spare reloads.

Optional `traverseLimitsDeg: [min, max]` defines mechanical travel containing neutral. Launch sectors must fit inside it; both simulation backends train through the interval rather than taking a shorter path across the stops. Omission preserves unrestricted rotation. The port articulation preview uses these limits.

A trainable assembly exports `<launcher-id>.yaw` with its `<tube-id>.muzzle` children intact. `ship:check` checks the pivot, parenting, neutral muzzle, and four broadside training poses. `ShipView` binds launcher states by stable ID and interpolates CPU yaw within mechanical limits when present, or through angle wrap otherwise; diagnostics compare every displayed tube origin against the same sampled pose. The development port articulation hook now previews torpedo training too.

`assets/parts/guns.json` optionally supplies `depthCharges`, and a blueprint's `depthChargeLaunchers` specifies each rack/thrower's stable ID, part, release position, ship-local launch velocity, magazine connection and total ammunition. Its compiled part supplies the dimensions, sinking rate, detonation depth, blast radius, damage, breach area, station reload and launch spacing. Every station exports `<launcher-id>.release`, checked against its authored position. Historical presets and future player ships use these definitions without ship-name branches in combat.

The renderer-free CPU integrates air travel with an exact sea-entry split, then a fixed sinking speed and horizontal water drag. Charges detonate once at their authored depth and leave the active projectile collection. Three-dimensional distance to an approximate submerged hull envelope determines quadratic damage falloff. Machinery/magazine damage, breaches, flooding, enemy damage score and frag attribution use shared combat state; allies, wrecks and the launching hull can also be affected. Blasts do not create AP decals. Bots release during predicted close passes only when the predicted blast clears friendly hulls. They retain ordinary surface-fleet maneuvering; sonar search and dedicated submarine-hunting tactics are absent.

The fitted weapon group's direct HUD shortcut selects depth charges (default **5** on Fletcher). A click releases one eligible station; held fire lays a spaced pattern. This weapon is independent of sight aim. Its instrument explains the release stations, burst depth and need to clear the blast. Depth-charge statistics come from the compiled part catalog. The visual adapter consumes air/submerged charge positions and splash/blast events for drums, water columns and foam; audio reuses the existing naval set. Pausing freezes motion and effects, and returning to port restores stocks and clears both underwater projectile types.

The initial Fletcher setting (10 m depth, 32 m radius) deliberately makes charges usable in the current surface game, including against Type VIIC. Detailed shock propagation, depth selection, hydrostatic fuze physics and sonar are not implemented. The source/discrepancy register separates historical weapon counts from these gameplay approximations. The new enclosed single-gun component also uses the shared barrel-ID contract; all existing ships were rebuilt after that recipe/compiler change.

## Diving equipment (optional version 1 extension)

A blueprint's optional `submarine` equipment defines submerged handling, ballast capacity and rates, neutral fill fraction, vertical speed limits, periscope eye/depth, depth and torpedo-launch limits, surface/submerged engine module IDs, and appendage joint IDs. The compiler validates finite bounded parameters, engine links, periscope placement and distinct joint IDs. Export checks require each declared appendage to remain an independent pivot empty with moving children. Rebuild affected registered presets when shared compiler code changes; the Bismarck baseline remains untouched.

`Combatant.submarine` holds serializable depth order, ballast volume, emergency blow, plane order and trim. `motion.y` remains the one world-space hull height. `stepSubmarine` follows flooding each fixed tick: the ballast/plane controller adjusts buoyancy and vertical velocity, then combines commanded pitch with damage trim. Surface immersion restores buoyancy; damage water adds weight without being confused with ballast. Intentional depth does not trigger sinking. Sinking hulls continue downward from their current depth and cannot be revived with a depth order. Breach inflow for submarines uses actual depth pressure; exceeding the operating limit grows a persistent keel breach adjacent to the central compartment at 0.0001 m² per second per excess metre. The opening participates in shared flooding, pumping and damage control. Engine availability uses the current diesel/electric group and shared immersion checks. If a submarine also has a stability profile, ballast retains depth control while the stability solver evaluates damage loads and righting moments. These are bounded gameplay hydrostatics, not a pressure-hull strength calculation.

Diesel and electric groups supply power in their respective modes; underwater handling uses the same movement solver with a different definition. Guns report `submerged` when secured; tubes report `too-deep` beyond the ship's launch limit. Projectiles and hull collisions use actual CPU poses. Torpedoes retain their straight course and settle to their own authored run depth after leaving the actual tube. Depth controls reset with the actor. Combat bots begin diving within 1.6 times their usable torpedo range, retain attack depth through reloads and turns, and surface beyond 1.8 times that range or when no usable torpedoes remain. Attack depth is capped by the authored torpedo-launch depth limit. Passive target modes remain surfaced. Shore avoidance preserves depth and emergency ballast orders.

The renderer follows the CPU hull height with the exterior and appendage joints. Submarine chase can pass under water; bridge/binoculars use the periscope eye and do not stay artificially above the sea when the scope is submerged. Tactical remains above water, and port keeps terrain clearance. Water Pro supplies the existing underwater attenuation; GPU waves never choose gameplay depth or buoyancy. Surface wake generation fades with hull depth. The compact Depth instrument exposes actual/ordered depth, vertical rate, ballast, propulsion and depth orders; U/J order surface/50 m, and Z/X/B adjust depth or blow ballast. These bindings use the existing configurable keybinding migration. P toggles the raised periscope optics on submarines without changing depth orders, with the same aim preservation and camera guards as binoculars; it is rebindable and exposed on the Depth instrument.

## Carrier air groups (optional version 1 extension)

The blueprint's `airWing` records version, flight-deck launch/recovery datums, service module, launch/rearm timers and bounded squadrons with stable IDs, model IDs, roles and inventory. Version 1 also accepts optional `flightSize` (1–6), `deckCapacity` (1–24), and `maxActiveFlights` (1–4). Existing blueprints default to three-plane flights, 18 deck positions (or the inventory if smaller), and four active flights. Each of up to three squadron pools accepts 1–36 aircraft, with at most 96 aboard. Enterprise authors 48 aircraft, six-plane flights, 12 deck positions and four active flights. These are gameplay capacity choices. The compiler validates these fields and model/role pairs. The current registered gameplay models are F4F-4, SBD-3 and TBD-1; enabling another model requires explicitly adding its gameplay support. Any future blueprint may fit this equipment; combat contains no Enterprise-name branch. The compiled definition, ship hash and rebuilt GLB remain one version. Existing aircraft assets are consumed unchanged.

The CPU owns individual aircraft and payloads. Launches use per-carrier deck and active-flight rules, with no global airborne count cap. Simulation aircraft origins are engine-shaft datums, with per-model tyre clearances over authored deck positions. `AircraftView` batches rigid component meshes without merging their joint owners and samples the published LODs at 120/400 m. See [air operations and limitations](air-operations.md).


#### Ballistic continuation and active collections

Ricochet contacts apply an inelastic reflection in the CPU hull frame and continue the remaining fixed tick; they retain an already armed fuze. Water entry/exit splits elapsed time at the CPU sea surface. Entry reduces velocity; a caliber-dependent effective drag coefficient then rapidly attenuates the underwater run and its penetration budget. Low-energy unarmed shells retire, while armed shells remain until their burst deadline. Nearby underwater hulls use the ordinary swept contacts and breach system. HE water entry initiates its burst. Water shock, cavity dynamics, tumbling, shell breakup and hydrodynamic skipping are not resolved. The coefficients are provisional, documented in `assets/reviews/naval-mechanics/README.md`.

Active shells, torpedoes, depth charges and aircraft have no global count-based launch gate. Finite ammunition, reload/launch intervals, per-carrier deck rules and physical weapon lifetimes still apply. GPU instances expand in bounded-size allocations; these allocation sizes do not limit the simulation population.

## Ensigns and radar rigs

The optional `rig.version: 1` blueprint extension declares `ensigns` and `radars`. Each ensign has a stable ID, a catalog design (`us-48`, `white-ensign`, `ijn`, `kriegsmarine`), hoist `position` in runtime coordinates, `width` in metres and `staffHeight`. Zero staff height reuses an existing original staff. `create_flagstaffs` in `scripts/ships/blender_rig.py` authors any required staff and the stable `<id>.hoist` socket; export validation checks its position. The original design recipe and references live under `assets/parts/ensigns*`.

Radar entries bind an explicit `nodeId`, with revolutions per minute and optional `sweepDeg` and `phaseDeg`. A sweep angle is the half-sector; its RPM controls sweep cycles per minute. Author the moving pieces under a retained pivot using `radar_pivot`, leaving fixed mast geometry outside it. Director-mounted aerials may share the director's pivot; KGV's original `radar-284.yaw` remains a child of `dct-forward.yaw`. Export rejects empty/missing radar joints. These joints participate in the renderer's moving-owner and fleet batching contracts.

`ShipRigView` attaches independently deforming cloth outside rigid fleet batches. `FlagCloth` integrates at 120 Hz, with gravity, relative airflow, aerodynamic pressure and fabric length/shear/bend constraints. Wind comes from the same weather settings as the ocean; hull velocity and yaw produce apparent wind. No GPU ocean sample changes a CPU combat pose. Radar sweeps are visual animation rather than targeting decisions. Their clock holds in port while cloth continues to respond to wind, and advances again after departure. Small projected flags suspend cloth work, accounting for binocular magnification. Pause, ship reset, inspection and resource disposal are shared with the owning ship view.

### Underwater protection (additive version 1 extension)

`underwaterProtection: { version: 1, basis, zones }` authors torpedo-defense coverage
in the same blueprint format for presets and future custom ships. Each zone has a
stable `id`, `name`, ship-local `center`/`size`, and `damageReduction` and
`breachReduction` fractions from 0 through 0.9. Zones must fit inside the submerged
hull envelope. Omitting the extension leaves a ship unprotected. These are defense
system calibration values, separate from armor thickness or shell penetration.

Armed torpedo contact first applies common tuning: 62.5% of authored damage and
7/11 of authored breach area. A zone containing the actual ship-local contact then
reduces hull/module damage and breach area independently. Overlapping zones use
the strongest reduction of each kind; they do not multiply. Bow, stern, opposite
side and keel contacts outside coverage receive no protection. Existing local
structural saturation, breach merging, physical flooding and scoring still apply.
Depth charges and bombs retain their existing damage rules.

Bismarck, Yamato and King George V initially use two estimated central side zones,
with 50% damage and breach reductions. Coverage and effectiveness are provisional
gameplay calibration, not certified historical measurements. Protection itself
does not degrade with repeated hits in this version; structural saturation and
accumulating floodwater still do. Selecting a defense row in Armor inspection shows its blue coverage volume,
reduction percentages and the estimated basis. Hit messages identify protected hits.
